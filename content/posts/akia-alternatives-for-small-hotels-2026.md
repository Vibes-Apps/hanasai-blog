Which one fits depends on whether you want the phone bundled with a messaging suite, whether you want to see a price before talking to sales, how much setup you can take on, and where call data is stored.

## What Akia does well, and who it suits

Fairness first. According to Akia's own site (read 18 November 2026), it is a guest-communication platform first. Its pricing page lists a unified inbox (SMS, email, web chat, OTA messages, WhatsApp and social), mini apps for digital check-in, arrival guides, tipping and menus, scheduled messaging and upsells. AI Voice is listed as part of the Max tier, with call transfers, call summaries and dedicated support.

Its homepage says the voice agent "picks up every call", holds a real conversation and takes payment before the call ends, and that Akia connects to dozens of property management systems (PMSs). The page source names Opera, Mews, Cloudbeds, Apaleo, StayNTouch and Streamline. Its security page, read on 17 November 2026, states SOC 2 Type II.

If you want guest messaging, digital check-in and the phone on one platform, and you are comfortable buying through a sales conversation, that is a real strength.

## Why a small hotel might look elsewhere

These are reasons, not faults. They depend on your hotel.

- **No price on the page.** Every tier says "Talk to Sales". Voice is only on the top tier, so a phone-only buyer may end up paying for a suite.
- **Tier limits.** The pricing page lists limits (journeys and touchpoints) per tier. We could not tell from the page how these affect a voice-only setup. Ask.
- **Voice details not stated.** The pages we read do not say how many languages the voice agent speaks, how many call minutes a tier includes, or where call data is stored.
- **Payment on the call.** A homepage claim is not the same as a documented process. Ask which payment provider is used and how card details are handled. In the UK that includes card-data rules; see our post on [whether a hotel AI should confirm bookings](https://gethanas.com/blog/should-an-ai-confirm-hotel-bookings-why-staff-approval-matters) for the staff-approval side.

## The alternatives, one by one

### Canary Technologies: a full platform with AI Voice

Canary describes itself as a hospitality platform with check-in, messaging, payments, sales and catering modules, and an AI Voice product with front-desk, reservations, concierge and booking assistants. Its pricing page offers custom pricing only. See the [Canary alternatives article](https://gethanas.com/blog/canary-technologies-alternatives-for-independent-hotels-2026) for the detail.

### Dextr AI: booking across calls, texts and chat

Dextr AI says it books across calls, texts and web chat, names several PMSs and uses a seven-day guided setup. Its homepage shows no price (read 17 November 2026). More in our [Dextr AI alternatives article](https://gethanas.com/blog/dextr-ai-alternatives-for-small-independent-hotels-2026).

### D3x: PMS-connected, with a public per-room price

D3x publishes an "Independent & SMB" tier at €5 per room a month for messaging, with phone AI as an add-on at €100 a month for 5 hours, then €12 per extra hour. Its [AI hotel receptionist page](https://www.d3x.ai/solutions/ai-hotel-receptionist) says the phone AI can look up and confirm changes to reservations in your PMS. Whether it creates new bookings, and where data is stored, are not stated on the pages we read. See [Hanas vs D3x](https://gethanas.com/compare/d3x).

### Motel4: self-serve, no PMS needed

Motel4's site describes a three-step setup of about 15 minutes: add property details, forward the front-desk line, go live. It says no PMS integration or new hardware is needed and that it captures booking enquiries with the caller's number. Pricing is a free 30 minutes a month, then $149, $299 and $599 a month, with a 30-day money-back guarantee. The minutes in each paid tier, and data location, are not stated.

### Hanas: public price, staff approval, early access

Hanas answers the phone at any hour in the caller's language, says it is an AI, quotes only the rates the hotel enters, and sends a booking request to staff to approve or decline. Setup starts from your website: it drafts a profile, and your team reviews it before anything goes live.

The price is on the page: £49 a month with 150 minutes, then £0.30 a minute (early-access price, excluding VAT, which the site says may change before general availability). The demo offers English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Russian and Arabic.

**Where Hanas is the weaker choice.** It has no PMS integration yet, so staff enter approved bookings themselves (Mews, Cloudbeds, Little Hotelier, Guestline and eviivo are on the roadmap, with no dates). By default it sends requests rather than confirming bookings, and by default it does not take payment. That is the current default and may change, but if you need either today, Akia, Dextr AI, Canary or D3x are better matches on their own claims. It is a phone product only, with no messaging inbox, check-in or guest-guide modules. Hanas is in early access with no customer reviews or case studies yet. See the [privacy notice](https://gethanas.com/privacy) and [Hanas for boutique hotels](https://gethanas.com/for/boutique-hotels).

## What 300 minutes a month costs, where prices allow it

Using only published prices, in each vendor's own currency:

- **Hanas:** £49 includes 150 minutes. The other 150 minutes at £0.30 add £45.00, which gives £94.
- **D3x, 20 rooms:** €5 x 20 rooms is €100, plus €100 for 5 phone hours (300 minutes), which gives about €200. This is our reading of two separate lines; confirm the total with D3x.
- **Akia, Canary, Dextr AI:** no public price, so no total.

Prices leave out VAT, setup and overage. Our [cost article](https://gethanas.com/blog/ai-receptionist-for-hotels-what-it-costs-in-the-uk-2026) shows the workings.

## How to choose

![Decision flow: for messaging and phone in one suite look at Akia or Canary; for PMS-written bookings look at Akia, Canary, Dextr AI or D3x; for a public price and staff approval look at Hanas or D3x; for self-serve setup with no PMS look at Motel4](/blog/images/akia-alternatives-small-hotels-akia-alternatives-choose.png)

_A simple way to narrow the field_

- **You want messaging, check-in and phone together:** Akia or Canary, on their own descriptions. Ask each for a quote at your room count.
- **You need bookings written into your PMS:** Akia, Canary, Dextr AI or D3x, according to their own claims. Confirm exactly which PMS and which actions.
- **You want a price you can model and a quick start:** D3x, Hanas or Motel4, depending on whether you need PMS connection.
- **You want a person to approve every booking:** Hanas does this by default. Others may offer it; not stated on the pages we read.

Whichever you choose, trial it on your own call types. Our post on [how many calls a small hotel misses](https://gethanas.com/blog/how-many-calls-does-a-small-hotel-miss-what-it-costs) shows how to measure first.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Akia publish its pricing?

No. Its pricing page, read on 18 November 2026, shows "Talk to Sales" on Basic, Pro and Max.

### Is AI Voice included in every Akia plan?

On the pricing page we read, AI Voice is listed under Max only. Confirm with the vendor, since plans can change.

### Does Akia take payment on the call?

Its homepage says the voice agent takes payment before hanging up. We have not tested this and the pricing page does not explain how it works. Ask for a demo with your payment setup.

### Do I need PMS integration?

Not necessarily. A request-based or enquiry-capture tool works alongside your PMS, with staff entering bookings. Integration saves staff time but usually means more setup.

### Where is call data stored?

None of the Akia pages we read state a location. Ask every vendor. For the rules, see [Is it legal to use an AI receptionist at a UK hotel?](https://gethanas.com/blog/is-it-legal-to-use-an-ai-receptionist-at-a-uk-hotel). This is not legal advice.
