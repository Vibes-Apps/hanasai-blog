A good hotel phone greeting does four jobs in about ten seconds: it says where the caller has reached, who or what is speaking, anything they need to know about the call, and how you can help. When an AI answers, we think the greeting should also say it is an AI. This post gives sample wording and the reasoning, and it is general guidance, not legal advice.

## What a greeting needs to do

Think of the caller. They have a question and a few seconds of patience. A greeting should answer, in order:

1. **Where am I?** The hotel's name, clearly, so a wrong number is spotted at once.
2. **Who am I speaking to?** A name for a person, or "AI receptionist" or "AI assistant" for a system.
3. **Is there anything I should know about this call?** Recording, or a reduced service such as "the front desk is closed".
4. **What happens next?** An open question: "How can I help?"

## Sample greetings

These are starting points. Adapt them to your hotel's voice.

**Staff, daytime**

> "Good morning, Harbour House, Sam speaking. How can I help?"

**Staff, busy period**

> "Harbour House, thank you for holding. Sam speaking."

**AI receptionist, any hour**

> "Hello, you've reached Harbour House. I'm an AI receptionist, and this call is recorded. How can I help?"

**AI receptionist, front desk closed**

> "Hello, you've reached Harbour House. The front desk is closed until seven, but I'm an AI receptionist and I can help with questions or take details for the team. This call is recorded. How can I help?"

"Harbour House" and "Sam" are made up for illustration. The last version works because it is honest about the situation before the caller starts explaining.

## Why an AI should say it is an AI

We are not aware of a settled rule that covers every hotel phone call, and we have not verified one for this article, so we do not present this as a legal requirement. Our reasons are practical:

- **Trust.** A caller who realises halfway through that they are talking to a machine may stop trusting what they were told, including rates and policies.
- **Fit.** If the caller knows it is an AI, they phrase things simply and expect a hand-over for complicated requests.
- **Consistency with recording.** If you record calls, you already have a reason to say something at the start. One extra phrase costs little.
- **Your reputation.** Small hotels live on reviews. A call that felt like a trick can end up in one.

Separately, if you handle personal data on calls, UK data protection rules apply; our guide to the [recording notice](https://gethanas.com/blog/call-recording-notice-for-hotels-what-to-say-at-the-start-of-a-call) covers what we read and what we did not.

## Things to avoid in a greeting

- **A long menu.** "Press one for reservations" is the IVR habit; see our post on [voice agents versus IVR and chatbots](https://gethanas.com/blog/ai-voice-agent-vs-ivr-vs-chatbot-for-hotels-what-is-the-difference).
- **A fake name for the AI.** A friendly name is fine, but not a human name that implies a person.
- **Promises you cannot keep.** "Your room is booked" should only be said if it is true. See [booking requests versus confirmed bookings](https://gethanas.com/blog/booking-request-vs-confirmed-booking-what-is-the-difference).
- **Legal paragraphs.** Put the detail on your website and keep the spoken notice to a sentence.
- **Jargon.** "Virtual agent powered by generative AI" is longer and clearer to nobody. "AI receptionist" is enough.

## How to test your greeting

1. Ring your own number from a mobile and listen once without knowing the script.
2. Ask a friend who has never stayed with you to do the same and say what they understood.
3. Check the greeting at different times: day, evening, front desk closed.
4. Time it. If it takes longer than about ten to fifteen seconds, cut it.
5. Re-listen after any change to hours, policies or the AI's settings.

## Greeting checklist

1. Hotel name said first and clearly.
2. Speaker identified: a name for staff, "AI receptionist" for an AI.
3. Recording mentioned if calls are recorded.
4. Reduced service mentioned if the desk is closed.
5. Ends with an open offer of help.
6. Under about fifteen seconds.
7. Tested by someone who did not write it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to say a call is recorded?

Our recording-notice post sets out what we read on the subject and what we did not. Check your own position, and take advice if unsure.

### Is it rude for an AI to answer a hotel phone?

It depends on the caller's expectations. Saying clearly at the start that it is an AI, and handing over to a person when needed, avoids most of the awkwardness.

### Should the AI have a name?

A name is fine if it does not suggest a human. "AI receptionist" on its own is simple and clear.

### What if the caller asks for a person?

Decide in advance what happens: a call-back request, a transfer to an on-call contact, or a note for the morning. The AI should say which.

### Should the greeting be in several languages?

Only if the system can really continue in those languages. See our guide to [foreign-language callers](https://gethanas.com/blog/how-to-handle-foreign-language-callers-at-a-small-uk-hotel).
