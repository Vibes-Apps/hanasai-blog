A night auditor is a hotel employee who works overnight, closes the day's accounts and looks after the front desk while most guests sleep. Larger hotels with a property management system (PMS) usually have one. Many small hotels and B&Bs do not: the owner, a live-in manager or an on-call member of staff covers the night, and the accounts are checked in the morning. Whether you need a night auditor depends on what has to happen overnight, not on the job title.

## What a night auditor does

The title comes from the "night audit": the end-of-day routine in which a hotel checks that the day's charges, payments and room status are correct and then closes the books for that business day. In a PMS, this is often a set of steps that runs when the day is rolled over, followed by reports that a person reviews.

In practice, the job usually mixes two kinds of work.

| Area | Typical tasks |
| --- | --- |
| **Accounting** | Check room charges and other postings, compare payments with the records, review the day's reports, roll the system to the next business day |
| **Front desk** | Late arrivals, guest requests, noise or safety issues, key problems, taking calls, early-morning departures and handing over to the day team |

Hotels differ widely in how they split these tasks, and some use a different title. The details above are general, not a fixed standard.

## Why larger hotels have one

A hotel with many rooms, a restaurant, a bar and several revenue points has a lot of transactions to reconcile each day, and guests who may arrive or need help at any hour. A dedicated person overnight does both jobs and hands over a clean set of accounts to the morning team.

A small hotel has fewer postings and fewer arrivals after midnight. That changes the maths, but it does not remove the questions.

## What small hotels do instead

There is no single right answer. These are common set-ups, each with trade-offs:

- **Owner or manager on call.** The person lives on site or nearby and takes the phone overnight. Cheap in cash terms, but the cost is the owner's sleep and holidays.
- **A live-in or night-shift employee.** Reliable cover for the desk, but a real staffing commitment, and you must plan for sickness and leave.
- **Morning review of the accounts.** The PMS or the booking records are checked the next day rather than at midnight. This works if you do not need the figures overnight and you are comfortable with that timing.
- **An answering service.** A person takes a message or follows a script. Useful for taking details; usually less able to answer detailed questions about your property. See our comparison of [AI voice agents, answering services and night porters](https://gethanas.com/blog/ai-voice-agent-vs-answering-service-vs-night-porter-which-fits-a-small-uk-hotel).
- **An AI receptionist.** Answers calls and, depending on the product, other channels, and passes anything urgent to a person. It does not do the accounting.

## Separate the tasks before you decide

Write down what actually needs to happen between the last evening check-in and breakfast, then assign each task. A simple way to do it:

1. **Late arrivals.** Who lets them in, and how do they get a key?
2. **Phone and messages.** Who answers, and what is the standard answer to common questions? Our [out-of-hours checklist](https://gethanas.com/blog/out-of-hours-call-handling-checklist-for-small-hotels) lists the situations to plan for.
3. **Urgent problems.** Who is called for a safety issue, a lock-out or a noise complaint, and how fast must they respond?
4. **Accounts.** Does anything need checking at midnight, or can it wait for the morning?
5. **Handover.** What does the morning team receive, and where?

Once the list is written, you can see which tasks need a person who is awake and on site, and which can be handled by a process, a tool or a morning check.

## A quick test: do you need a dedicated night auditor?

Answer yes or no for each question:

1. Do guests regularly arrive after the desk would normally close?
2. Do you need the day's figures checked overnight, not in the morning?
3. Do you have transactions, such as a bar or restaurant, that must be reconciled daily?
4. Do you expect safety or access problems that need someone on site?

Mostly no usually points to a lighter set-up, such as an on-call owner plus a way to take calls and a morning review. Mostly yes points to a person on site overnight. This is a thinking aid, not a rule, and your own circumstances and any obligations to staff or guests come first.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a night auditor the same as a night porter?

The titles overlap, and hotels use them differently. In general, a night auditor leans towards the accounting routine and a night porter towards the desk, luggage and security, but one person may do both. Check what the role in your hotel actually covers.

### Can a small hotel run without anyone awake overnight?

Some do, with clear instructions to guests, a key safe or self-check-in and an emergency number that reaches a person. Whether this suits you depends on your guests, your building and any requirements that apply to your property. We have not covered fire safety or licensing here, so check those separately.

### Does an AI receptionist replace a night auditor?

Not by itself. It can cover calls and some messages, but the accounting and any physical presence need another solution.

### Can the PMS night audit run without a person?

Some systems can run parts of it automatically, but a person should still review the reports. Ask your PMS vendor what is automated and what is not.

### What should I do first?

Write the overnight task list above. It shows which jobs need a person and which do not, before you spend anything.
