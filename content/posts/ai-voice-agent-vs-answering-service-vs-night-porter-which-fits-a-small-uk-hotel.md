## Which option fits a small UK hotel?

It depends on what you need covered, and when. A night porter is the only option that is physically in the building, so it is the only one that deals with a guest at the door, a locked-out guest or an emergency on site. A human answering service is a good fit if you only want calls answered and messages passed on, at any hour. An AI voice agent suits properties that want calls answered in several languages, with questions answered from your own information and a booking request logged for staff to approve. Many small hotels combine two. Detailed price workings are in [our cost article](https://gethanas.com/blog/ai-receptionist-for-hotels-what-it-costs-in-the-uk-2026).

## Start with the question that matters: what must be covered?

Before comparing products, note three things for your own property: which hours are unstaffed, what callers actually ask (rates, check-in, changes, complaints, in-stay problems), and what happens if nobody is there at 2am. A phone service of any kind works on calls only. A night porter works on calls and on everything else that happens in a building at night. Mixing those two jobs up is the most common mistake in this comparison.

Two rows are worth a longer look.

**Emergencies.** The Hanas site does not describe emergency handling or a live transfer to a person, so we do not claim it. If a guest rings about a fire alarm, a medical problem or a break-in, a phone service cannot go to the room. If you use any phone service out of hours, agree in writing what an urgent call does, and test it.

**Several languages.** An AI agent can answer in a guest's language at any hour, which a small team cannot easily match. The Hanas site lists seven languages in its demo and says it can switch to five more mid-call, while stating that it does not guarantee any language and that quality varies. Try your most common languages before committing. Ask the answering services about language coverage too.

## The AI voice agent: who it suits, and when it is the weaker choice

An AI voice agent is software that answers a phone call and holds a conversation. Hanas answers in the guest's language, tells the caller it is an assistant and that the call is recorded, and passes bookings and callback requests to your staff to approve, or confirms bookings automatically if the owner sets it that way.

**It suits** properties where:

- most missed calls are routine: rates, availability, check-in time, parking, pets, directions;
- the unstaffed hours are long, or staff are often busy when calls arrive;
- guests call from abroad and speak other languages;
- you want a written record of each request to follow up in the morning.

**It is the weaker choice** when:

- **A caller needs judgement or empathy.** Complaints, weddings and group bookings suit a person.
- **You need the booking written into your PMS on the call.** Hanas can confirm a booking itself if the owner sets it to, but it does not yet connect to a PMS, so staff enter bookings themselves. Other vendors do offer PMS-connected booking, which we cover in our [comparison of seven AI receptionists](https://gethanas.com/blog/7-best-ai-receptionists-for-uk-small-hotels-and-b-bs-2026).
- **Data location matters to you.** Hanas stores transcripts in Ireland and deletes them after 30 days by default, but its speech provider, ElevenLabs, processes calls in the US. It is also an early-access product with no published customer case studies yet.

## The human answering service: who it suits

A UK answering service puts a person on your line who answers in your business name and passes on details. TTNC's page describes taking name, number and reason for calling by email, and says its team is UK-based and answers 24/7 at no extra charge for out-of-hours calls. It also says its team can answer a variety of questions, take orders and book appointments, depending on the script you supply.

**It suits** properties that:

- want a human voice on every call but cannot staff the hours;
- take few, high-value calls where a message and a quick call-back are enough, such as a small B&B with a handful of calls a week;
- need short-term cover. TTNC says its service is monthly rolling and can be used seasonally.

**It is the weaker choice** when you get many calls, because you pay by the minute (see the numbers below). The script has to carry your rooms, rates and local knowledge, and it does not write into your booking system. Ask the provider for language coverage, call recording and how urgent calls are escalated, because we could not confirm them from the pages we read.

## The night porter: who it suits

A night porter is awake and on site: they check in late arrivals, answer the door and phone, deal with noise and alarms, and can act in person.

**It suits** properties where:

- there is a regular flow of late arrivals or guests who need help overnight;
- the building is large, has complex fire or safety arrangements, or a bar or event space that runs late;
- your fire risk assessment assumes someone is awake on site. GOV.UK says the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order applies if you have paying guests, including a bed and breakfast or guesthouse, and that you, as the responsible person, must carry out a fire risk assessment. Read your own assessment before changing night cover. See [GOV.UK on fire safety in the workplace](https://www.gov.uk/workplace-fire-safety-your-responsibilities).

**It is the weaker choice** when nights are quiet: you pay for eight hours of presence to handle a handful of calls, and holiday and sickness cover is your problem.

## What it costs, in short

We keep the full tables and workings in [our cost article](https://gethanas.com/blog/ai-receptionist-for-hotels-what-it-costs-in-the-uk-2026). In summary, using each vendor's own published prices excluding VAT:

- **AI voice agent.** Hanas is £49 a month including 150 minutes, then £0.30 a minute, an early-access price that may change. At 300 minutes that is £94.
- **Answering service.** TTNC publishes £12.66 a month plus £1.20 a minute, which is £372.66 for 300 minutes. Posh's Elegant plan is £128 a month with 100 minutes, then £1.35 a minute (prices effective 1 April 2026), which is £398 on our workings for 300 minutes. Minutes are counted differently by different providers.
- **Night porter.** The National Living Wage for those aged 21 and over is [£12.71 an hour from April 2026](https://www.gov.uk/national-minimum-wage-rates). Adding employer National Insurance and a minimum employer pension, our workings in the cost article come to about £14.50 to £15.50 an hour. One overnight shift every night is roughly £42,000 to £45,000 a year before holiday cover, recruitment and training. That is our estimate, not a quote, and the Employment Allowance may reduce employer National Insurance for eligible employers.

These are not like for like: the first two cover calls only, while a night porter does much more. Compare the job you need done, not the totals.

## Call recording and your duties

Whichever option you choose, whoever answers your calls handles guests' personal data on your behalf, and you remain responsible for telling callers what happens. For the rules on recording calls and telling people, see the [ICO guidance on monitoring telephone calls](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/employment/monitoring-workers/specific-data-protection-considerations-for-different-ways-or-methods-of-monitoring-workers/) and the [Lawful Business Practice Regulations 2000](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2000/2699/regulation/3), and read our [legal guide](https://gethanas.com/blog/is-it-legal-to-use-an-ai-receptionist-at-a-uk-hotel). This is not legal advice. In practice, ask each provider whether calls are recorded, where recordings and transcripts are stored, for how long, and whether they sign a data processing agreement.

## Which one fits? A decision guide

Work through these in order. Stop at the first line that clearly describes you.

1. **Guests may need help in person overnight, or your fire risk assessment relies on someone being awake.** Keep a night porter or other on-site cover. A phone service can sit alongside it, but cannot replace it.
2. **Nights are unattended anyway and you only want a message.** Few calls, mostly bookings you will return in the morning: a human answering service or an AI agent will both work. At low volumes the cost difference is small, so choose on how you want calls to sound.
3. **You get many routine calls, or many in other languages.** An AI agent is usually cheaper per call on published prices and answers in more languages. A human service at £1.20 or more a minute rises quickly with volume.
4. **Calls are complex, emotional or high value** (weddings, groups, complaints). Use a person, either your own staff or a human service with a careful script.
5. **You need bookings written straight into your PMS.** Hanas does not do this yet. Look at vendors that state PMS integration, or keep staff in the loop.

[Count your missed calls for a week](https://gethanas.com/blog/how-many-calls-does-a-small-hotel-miss-what-it-costs) and note what callers asked: that is better evidence than any rule of thumb.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an AI voice agent replace a night porter?

Not as a whole. It answers calls and logs requests. It cannot let a guest in, check a room or respond to an alarm. If your night porter mainly answers the phone, call cover may be enough, but confirm that against your fire risk assessment and your guests' needs first.

### What happens to an emergency call if I use an AI agent?

That depends on the product and how you set it up. The Hanas site does not describe emergency handling, so we would not rely on it for one. Ask any vendor what happens to an urgent call, and give guests a direct emergency number in their booking confirmation. Fire and medical emergencies should go to 999.
