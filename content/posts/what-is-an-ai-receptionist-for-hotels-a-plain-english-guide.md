An AI receptionist for hotels is software that answers the hotel's phone with a synthetic voice, understands what the caller says, replies in conversation and passes the outcome to staff. It is not a person, and a good one says so at the start of the call. What differs between products is what happens after the conversation: some only take a message, some send a booking request for a human to approve, and some say they can write a booking straight into your property management system (PMS) or take a card payment.

## The short definition

An AI receptionist (also called an AI voice agent, AI phone agent, AI hotel receptionist or AI front desk) has three parts:

1. **Speech in and out.** It turns the caller's speech into text, decides what to say, and speaks the reply in a synthetic voice.
2. **Knowledge about your hotel.** Room types, opening hours, policies and rates, taken from your website or entered by you.
3. **A way to hand over.** An email, dashboard, message or system entry that tells your team what the caller wanted.

Everything else, such as languages, bookings and payment, is a feature layered on top.

## What it does on a typical call

A caller rings at 9pm asking whether a double room is free next Friday and whether dogs are allowed. The agent greets the caller, says it is an AI, answers the dog question from your policy, and takes the dates and contact details. What happens next depends on the product:

- **Message only:** it records the enquiry and emails it.
- **Request for approval:** it sends a booking request that a member of staff approves or declines.
- **Booking in your system:** it checks availability in your PMS and creates the booking. Vendors that offer this say it needs a PMS connection.
- **Payment:** it takes a deposit or card details on the call. Vendors that offer this need a payment set-up and have to handle card data carefully.

## What it is not

- **Not a chatbot.** A chatbot works in text on a website or app. A voice agent works on the phone line. Some vendors sell both.
- **Not a phone menu (IVR).** A menu says "press 1 for reservations". A voice agent lets the caller say what they want in their own words.
- **Not an answering service.** A traditional answering service uses human operators who take messages. An AI agent costs differently and does not tire, but it is software and can misunderstand. ## What it can and cannot do well

Fairly stated, on how vendors describe the category:

- **Usually good at:** answering common questions from set information, handling calls at any hour, taking down details, and speaking several languages.
- **Where care is needed:** unusual requests, noisy lines, strong accents, and anything where the answer is not in the information you provided. A sensible product hands off or takes a message instead of guessing.
- **Where the law applies:** call recording, personal data and, for bookings, consumer rules. We cover this in [Is it legal to use an AI receptionist at a UK hotel?](https://gethanas.com/blog/is-it-legal-to-use-an-ai-receptionist-at-a-uk-hotel) and [Should an AI confirm hotel bookings?](https://gethanas.com/blog/should-an-ai-confirm-hotel-bookings-why-staff-approval-matters). ## Five questions to ask any vendor

1. **Does it connect to my PMS, and which one?** Without a connection, staff enter bookings themselves. With one, ask which actions it can read and write. Our post on [using an AI receptionist without a PMS](https://gethanas.com/blog/ai-receptionist-without-a-pms-how-it-works-alongside-your-booking-system) explains the first case.
2. **Is the price published?** Some vendors list prices; others say "talk to sales". See [what an AI receptionist costs in the UK](https://gethanas.com/blog/ai-receptionist-for-hotels-what-it-costs-in-the-uk-2026).
3. **How much setup is it?** Ask what you must do before the first live call, and who does it.
4. **Where is call data stored?** Ask where transcripts and recordings are kept, for how long, and which other companies process audio.
5. **How does a call reach a person?** Ask what triggers a handoff and what the caller hears.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an AI receptionist the same as a chatbot?

No. A chatbot handles text on a website or app. An AI receptionist handles spoken calls on your phone line.

### Does it replace my staff?

It covers calls staff cannot take, such as at night or while checking guests in. A person still approves or acts on what it passes on, unless you choose a product and settings that act on their own.

### Do callers know it is an AI?

They should. A well-set-up agent says so at the start of the call. Check this in any trial.

### Can it take bookings and payments?

Some vendors say their agents can. Others, by default, send a request for staff to approve. Ask which, and test it on your own call types.

### Do I need a PMS connection?

Not necessarily. A request-based tool works alongside your PMS. A connection saves staff time but usually means more setup.

### How do I try one safely?

Use a trial on your own number, run your usual call types, and read the transcripts yourself. Our post on [how many calls a small hotel misses](https://gethanas.com/blog/how-many-calls-does-a-small-hotel-miss-what-it-costs) shows how to measure before you start.
