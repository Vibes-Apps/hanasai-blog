An AI receptionist is not "GDPR compliant" as a label you can buy. Compliance depends on how the product is set up and on how your hotel uses it. When a hotel hands calls to a supplier, the hotel is normally the one deciding why and how callers' personal data is used, and the ICO says that organisation stays responsible for choosing a supplier that gives "sufficient guarantees" and for having the right contract in place. This guide covers that part and gives a checklist of questions to ask a vendor. It is general information, not legal advice.

## What "GDPR compliant" can and cannot mean

When a vendor says its product is GDPR compliant, it usually means it has built features and policies to help customers comply. That is useful, but it cannot make your hotel compliant by itself. Your hotel still decides things such as which calls are recorded, how long you keep records and who can see them.

So treat the phrase as a prompt for questions, not an answer. This article uses the UK GDPR, which is the version that applies in the UK; hotels that also deal with other countries may face other rules.

## Who is responsible: the hotel and the supplier

Data protection law separates roles. In simple terms, a **controller** decides why and how personal data is processed, and a **processor** processes it on the controller's behalf. A hotel that uses an AI receptionist to handle its calls is typically the controller, and the vendor is typically a processor, but this depends on the facts and on the contract. If a processor acts outside the controller's instructions and decides the purposes and means itself, the ICO says it will be treated as a controller for that processing.

The practical point: using a supplier does not move your responsibility to the supplier. The ICO's guidance on contracts says a controller must only use a processor that can give "sufficient guarantees" to implement appropriate technical and organisational measures, and it is for the controller to satisfy itself that this is the case.

## The contract: what the ICO says it must cover

The ICO's guidance explains that under Article 28(3) of the UK GDPR, the contract between a controller and a processor must set out details of the processing: the subject matter and duration, its nature and purpose, the type of personal data and categories of people, and the controller's obligations and rights. It must also include minimum terms. In plain English, they are:

| Term | What it means for a hotel |
| --- | --- |
| **Documented instructions** | The vendor uses call data only as you instruct, and instructions can be in writing, including email |
| **Confidentiality** | Anyone at the vendor with access to the data is bound by a duty of confidence |
| **Security** | The vendor takes appropriate security measures |
| **Sub-processors** | The vendor does not use another company to process the data without your written authorisation, and passes on the same obligations |
| **People's rights** | The vendor helps you respond to requests such as access or deletion |
| **Helping you comply** | Help with security, breach notification and impact assessments where needed |
| **End of contract** | At your choice, data is deleted or returned, and copies deleted unless the law requires keeping them |
| **Audits** | The vendor provides the information to show compliance and allows audits and inspections |

The ICO also notes that standard contract clauses can be used, and that its guidance on this is under review. If your vendor offers a data processing agreement, read it against the list above rather than assuming it is complete.

## Questions to ask any AI receptionist vendor

Use these as a starting list and write down the answers:

1. **Contract.** Do you offer a written data processing agreement, and does it cover the terms above?
2. **Sub-processors.** Which other companies handle our callers' data, and will you tell us before that changes?
3. **Where data is stored.** In which countries is data stored and processed, and what safeguards apply if it leaves the UK? We have not covered international transfers in detail here.
4. **Recordings and transcripts.** Are calls recorded or transcribed, who can access them and can we turn this off?
5. **Retention.** How long is data kept, and can we choose?
6. **Deletion.** What happens to our data when we leave?
7. **People's requests.** How will you help us respond if a caller asks for their data or its deletion?
8. **Security.** What security measures do you use, and can you share your relevant policies?
9. **Incidents.** How and how quickly will you tell us if there is a data breach?
10. **Training.** Is our callers' data used to train or improve your models, and can we opt out?

The ICO guidance suggests controllers consider factors such as the supplier's compliance with industry standards, technical expertise and documentation like privacy and information security policies. A vendor who answers clearly and in writing is a better sign than one who points to a badge.

## What your hotel still has to do

Even with a good supplier, your hotel has its own work:

- **Tell callers** what happens to their data. See our guides on the [greeting](https://gethanas.com/blog/hotel-phone-greeting-what-to-say-and-why-an-ai-should-say-it-is-an-ai) and the [recording notice](https://gethanas.com/blog/call-recording-notice-for-hotels-what-to-say-at-the-start-of-a-call).
- **Decide what is recorded and kept**, and set a sensible retention period.
- **Limit who can see** calls, transcripts and requests.
- **Keep the contract and your checks on file.**
- **Take advice** on lawful basis, impact assessments and anything unusual, because we have not covered them here.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there such a thing as a GDPR-certified AI receptionist?

We have not found a general certification that makes a hotel compliant. The ICO mentions approved codes of conduct and certification schemes as a way a processor can show guarantees, when they are available, but it describes them as something to consider, not a requirement. Check what a vendor means by any badge.

### Do I need a contract with an AI receptionist vendor?

If the vendor processes personal data on your behalf, the ICO says a written contract covering the Article 28 terms is needed. Ask your adviser how this applies to your set-up.

### Can I use the vendor's standard terms?

Possibly, if they cover the required points. Read them against the list above and ask for changes where they do not.

### Does the vendor's own privacy policy cover my hotel?

A vendor's privacy policy describes how the vendor handles data. It does not replace your own notice to callers or your contract with the vendor.

### Does this apply outside the UK?

This guide covers the UK GDPR. Other countries have their own rules, which we have not covered.
