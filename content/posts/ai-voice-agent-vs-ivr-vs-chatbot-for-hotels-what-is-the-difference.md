An AI voice agent holds a spoken conversation with a caller, an IVR (interactive voice response) plays a menu and waits for the caller to press keys or say a set word, and a chatbot works in text on a website or app. All three can "answer" a hotel's enquiries, but only the first two sit on the phone line, and only the voice agent lets the caller say what they want in their own words.

> **Disclosure.** We make Hanas, an AI receptionist for small hotels, which is a voice agent. We describe the three categories in general terms and say where a voice agent is the weaker choice. We did not test any other product for this article.**Key Takeaways**The three differ in channel and in how the guest speaks: a voice agent is spoken conversation on the phone, an IVR is a menu on the phone, a chatbot is text on a screen.An IVR is predictable and cheap to reason about, but callers can get stuck in menus. A voice agent is flexible, but can misunderstand.A chatbot does nothing for a guest who is already ringing your number.They are not exclusive: many hotels use a chatbot on the website and a voice agent or menu on the phone.

## What an IVR is

An IVR is the familiar "press 1 for reservations, press 2 for the restaurant" menu. The caller chooses from options the hotel recorded in advance. Some IVRs accept spoken words such as "reservations", but the options are still fixed.

**Where it works:** a few clear destinations, such as reception, restaurant and spa, where every caller knows which one they want.

**Where it struggles:** a caller whose question does not fit a menu item, a caller on a mobile who cannot press keys easily, and anyone who just wants to ask "do you take dogs?". The menu cannot answer; it can only route.

## What a chatbot is

A chatbot is a text assistant, usually a box on a website or inside a messaging app. It answers typed questions from information you gave it. It can be useful for guests already browsing your site.

It does not answer your phone. A guest who finds your number on a map listing and rings it never meets the chatbot. Some vendors sell both a chatbot and a voice agent, but they are separate channels.

## What an AI voice agent is

A voice agent is software on the phone line. It turns speech into text, decides what to say from information you gave it, and replies in a synthetic voice. A caller can ask in their own words, in whatever order they like.

Three things matter more than the label:

1. **What it knows.** It should answer only from your information, and take a message when the answer is not there.
2. **What it does at the end of the call.** Take a message, send a booking request for staff to approve, write a booking into your property management system (PMS) or take payment. Our post on [what an AI receptionist for hotels is](https://gethanas.com/blog/what-is-an-ai-receptionist-for-hotels-a-plain-english-guide) covers these endings.
3. **Whether it says it is an AI.** A guest should not have to guess.

## Side by side: one call, three outcomes

Take a call at 9pm: "Is a double free next Friday, and do you allow dogs?"

- **IVR:** "Press 1 for reservations." The hotel is closed, so the call may go to a recorded message or voicemail. The caller's question about dogs is not answered.
- **Chatbot:** not involved. The caller used the phone.
- **Voice agent:** answers the dog policy from the hotel's information, takes dates and contact details, and passes the request on. What it does with the dates depends on the product.

## Where each one fits a small hotel

Our reading, as a vendor with a stake in the answer:

- **IVR fits** when a person always answers during opening hours and you only need to route a few call types out of hours. It is the simplest to reason about because nothing is improvised.
- **Chatbot fits** when many guests find you on your website and your phone volume is low. It does not reduce missed calls.
- **Voice agent fits** when calls arrive while staff are busy with guests or the desk is closed, and callers ask varied questions. It needs good information from you, and its answers should be read in transcripts, especially at the start.

Combinations are common. A chatbot on the site plus a voice agent on the phone covers both doors. An IVR in front of a voice agent adds a step most callers do not want. We do not have a measured figure for how many callers hang up at a menu, so we will not give one.

## Five questions to ask a vendor

1. **What can the agent do at the end of a call?** Message, request, booking in the PMS or payment?
2. **What does it do when it does not know?** Ask to hear a call where the answer is not in the information.
3. **How does a call reach a person?** What triggers the handoff and what does the caller hear?
4. **Does it say it is an AI?** Listen to the opening of a real call.
5. **Where is call data stored, and for how long?** See our guide on [UK legal issues for hotel AI receptionists](https://gethanas.com/blog/is-it-legal-to-use-an-ai-receptionist-at-a-uk-hotel). It is general information, not legal advice.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an AI voice agent just a smarter IVR?

No. An IVR routes callers through fixed options. A voice agent understands free speech and answers from your information. It can still fail on unusual questions, so ask what it does then.

### Can a hotel use a chatbot instead of a voice agent?

Only for guests who use the website. A chatbot does not answer the phone.

### Is an IVR cheaper?

It can be simple to run, but we have no price comparison to give, and prices vary by provider. Ask for published prices and be wary of per-minute charges you cannot forecast. Our post on [what an AI receptionist costs in the UK](https://gethanas.com/blog/ai-receptionist-for-hotels-what-it-costs-in-the-uk-2026) shows how to compare.

### Do callers prefer a person?

Many may. We do not have a source for how many, so we will not guess. Test with your own callers and always give a way to reach staff.

### Which is best for out-of-hours calls?

A voice agent or an answering service answers out of hours; a menu only routes. Our post on [AI voice agent vs answering service vs night porter](https://gethanas.com/blog/ai-voice-agent-vs-answering-service-vs-night-porter-which-fits-a-small-uk-hotel) compares the options for a small UK hotel.
