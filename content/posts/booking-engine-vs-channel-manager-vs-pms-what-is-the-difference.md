A booking engine sells your rooms to guests on your own website, a channel manager keeps your availability and rates in step across online travel agents and other sales channels, and a property management system (PMS) runs the hotel's day-to-day operations. Many products bundle two or all three, which is why the terms get mixed up. This guide separates the jobs.

## The three jobs in one sentence each

| System | Main job | Who uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Booking engine** | Lets guests search and book on your own website | Guests (front end), staff (settings) |
| **Channel manager** | Pushes availability and rates to online travel agents and other channels, and pulls bookings back | Revenue or reservations staff |
| **PMS** | Holds reservations, rooms and guest records; runs check-in, billing and housekeeping | Front desk and management |

## Booking engine

A booking engine is the part of your website where a guest picks dates, sees rooms and rates, and books. It is your direct channel, so it matters for hotels that want to depend less on third-party commissions.

What to look at: how it looks on a phone, which payment options it supports, whether it shows only the rooms that are really free, and whether it connects to your PMS so a booking arrives without retyping.

## Channel manager

If you list rooms on online travel agents, each listing needs current availability and rates. A channel manager updates them all from one place, so when a room sells on one channel it is closed on the others.

What to look at: which channels it connects to, how quickly updates reach them, how it handles rate rules, and what happens when a connection fails. Ask who charges for each connection.

## Property management system (PMS)

The PMS is the hotel's record of who is staying when and in which room. It supports check-in, check-out, billing, housekeeping and reports. For more, see our [guide to the PMS](https://gethanas.com/blog/what-is-a-property-management-system-pms-a-guide-for-small-hotels).

What to look at: how easy it is for your team to learn, what it connects to, and what you can export if you ever change system.

## Where they overlap

Real products blur the lines:

- Some PMS products include a booking engine, a channel manager, or both.
- Some channel managers include a booking engine.
- Some booking engines are sold as a module of a larger suite.

If a vendor says "all-in-one", ask what the three parts are called, whether each can be used alone, and what happens if you want to replace one later.

## Why it matters: double bookings and gaps

When two systems both think a room is free, two guests can book it. The usual protection is a single source of truth, normally the PMS, with the channel manager and booking engine reading from it in near real time. A small hotel that uses a spreadsheet, a separate booking engine and a few OTA extranets can manage, but someone has to update each by hand, and that is where mistakes creep in. We have no measured figure for how often double bookings happen and will not invent one.

## Where an AI receptionist fits

A tool that answers calls is separate from all three. It talks to callers; the three systems above store and sell inventory. How it hands bookings over varies:

- It may read availability from your PMS.
- It may work from rates you enter and pass a request to your team.
- It may write straight into your PMS, if it has that connection.

## Questions to ask a vendor

1. Which of the three jobs does your product do, and which do you only connect to?
2. What is included in the price, and what costs extra?
3. Which systems do you connect to today (not planned)?
4. Does data flow one way or both ways, live or batched?
5. What can I export if I leave?

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need all three?

Not necessarily. A tiny property may need none. A hotel selling on several channels usually benefits from all three jobs being covered, whether in one product or several.

### Is a channel manager the same as an OTA extranet?

No. An extranet is the login each travel agent gives you for your listing there. A channel manager updates many of them from one place.

### Can a booking engine work without a PMS?

Yes, but someone has to move bookings into wherever you manage the hotel, and keep availability aligned.

### Which should I buy first?

It depends on how you sell today. Start from the problem: double bookings, commission costs or time spent updating listings. We do not rank products in this post.

### Where does a voice AI fit?

It sits on the phone line and may connect to a PMS, or hand requests to staff. See above.
