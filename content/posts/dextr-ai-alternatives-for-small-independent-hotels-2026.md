If you run a small independent hotel, B&B or inn and are looking at Dextr AI, the alternatives worth comparing are D3x (PMS-connected, public per-room price), Motel4 (self-serve, no PMS), Conduit and HiJiffy (platforms that also cover guest messaging), Akia (claims booking and payment on the call) and Hanas. Which one fits depends mostly on four things: whether you need bookings written into your property management system (PMS), whether you want to see a price before talking to sales, how much setup you can take on, and where call data is stored.

## What Dextr AI does well, and who it suits

Fairness first. According to its own site (read 17 November 2026), Dextr AI is more than a phone product. It lists AI Reservations ("Book every call, text and web chat, 24/7"), guest experience, operations, direct marketing and group-sales email agents. It names Cloudbeds, Hostaway, Mews, ResNexus, Stayntouch and Track PMS, and its example flow shows a card verified with a pre-authorisation through Stripe. It says the AI hands edge cases to your team. Its customer types include hotels, resorts, campgrounds, vacation rentals and reservation contact centres.

If you want an AI that books into your PMS across several channels and you are happy with a guided rollout, that is a real strength. Its homepage describes the setup as a seven-day plan: kickoff on day 1, knowledge upload, PMS and Stripe sync, calibration, go-live on day 7.

## Why a small hotel might look elsewhere

These are reasons, not faults. They depend on your hotel.

- **No price on the page.** The Dextr AI homepage states no pricing. Its pricing, security and blog pages returned an access error when we tried to read them, so we cannot say what they contain. Ask the vendor.
- **Guided setup.** A kickoff-led plan suits a group with a systems owner. A one-person B&B may prefer something it can configure alone.
- **Breadth.** If you only need the phone answered, a platform covering marketing, operations and group sales may be more than you need.
- **Data location.** Not stated. The homepage lists GDPR and PCI, and "SOC 2 Type II (in progress)".

## The alternatives, one by one

### D3x: PMS-connected, with a public per-room price

D3x publishes an "Independent & SMB" tier at €5 per room a month for messaging, with phone AI as an add-on at €100 a month for 5 hours, then €12 per extra hour. Prices can be shown in EUR, USD or GBP. Its [AI hotel receptionist page](https://www.d3x.ai/solutions/ai-hotel-receptionist) says the phone AI can look up and confirm changes to reservations in your PMS and names Mews, Oracle Opera, Cloudbeds and Apaleo. Whether it creates new bookings, and where data is stored, are not stated on the pages we read. Setup is demo-led. See [Hanas vs D3x](https://gethanas.com/compare/d3x).

### Motel4: self-serve, no PMS needed

Motel4's site describes a three-step setup of about 15 minutes: add property details, forward the front-desk line, go live. It says no PMS integration or new hardware is needed. It captures booking enquiries with the caller's number. Whether it confirms bookings or takes payment is not stated, and neither is data location. It names 10 spoken languages without listing them. Pricing is a free 30 minutes a month, then Starter $149, Pro $299 and Business $599, with a 30-day money-back guarantee. The minutes in each paid tier are not stated.

### Conduit: voice and messaging on one platform

Conduit's pricing page shows Messaging at $18, Guest at $30 and Full Suite at $48 per unit a month, plus an $899 a month Startup plan for 0 to 25 units. Messaging lists "Voice AI, inbound and outbound", and higher tiers include everything in the tier below. Whether Startup includes voice is not explicitly stated. Booking and payment on calls, data location and named PMSs are not stated on the page we read. A per-unit price is harder to model if you only want calls answered.

### Akia: claims booking and payment on the call

Akia's pricing page lists no prices: tiers called Basic, Pro and Max each have a "Talk to Sales" button. It lists AI Voice, with call transfers and summaries, under Max only. Its homepage, as we read it on 15 November 2026 (not re-read today), claimed reservations and payment on the call and a long PMS list. Confirm this with the vendor, along with which tier includes the voice agent. Its [security page](https://www.akia.com/security) states SOC 2 Type II. Data location is not stated.

### HiJiffy: EU hosting, voice plus chat

HiJiffy's pricing is per room (per bed for hostels), shown as starting prices. Basic starts at £99 a month and Pro at £159, with a second set of figures (£105 and £170) on the same page; the page does not say which applies to which billing. Voice is listed under Pro as "Voice & Text", with 2 voice languages in Pro and 6 in Premium. Setup is a one-off fee per five properties. Its [security page](https://www.hijiffy.com/security-and-compliance) says servers are in Ireland on AWS and lists subprocessors including AWS, Google Cloud, Twilio and OpenAI (for AI add-ons only). We did not find a GDPR statement on that page today, so ask. Booking and payment on calls are not stated. Per-room pricing needs careful arithmetic for your room count.

### Hanas: public price, owner-set control, early access

Hanas answers the phone at any hour in the caller's language, says it is an AI, quotes only the rates the hotel enters, and either sends a booking request to staff to approve or confirms it automatically, depending on how the owner sets it. Hanas is built as an orchestrator: beyond calls it is designed to handle DMs, WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook and a CRM, with channels rolling out during early access. Setup starts from your website: it drafts a profile, and your team reviews it before anything goes live.

The price is on the page: £49 a month with 150 minutes, then £0.30 a minute (early-access price, excluding VAT, which the site says may change before general availability). Higher tiers exist. The demo offers English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Russian and Arabic, which is seven languages.

**Where Hanas is the weaker choice.** It has no PMS integration yet, so staff enter bookings themselves (Mews, Cloudbeds, Little Hotelier, Guestline and eviivo are on the roadmap, with no dates). If you need PMS-written bookings today, Dextr AI, D3x or Akia are better matches on their own claims. Hanas is in early access with no customer reviews or case studies yet. Transcripts are stored in Ireland and deleted after 30 days by default, but the speech provider, ElevenLabs, processes calls in the US. See the [privacy notice](https://gethanas.com/privacy) and [Hanas for boutique hotels](https://gethanas.com/for/boutique-hotels).

## What 300 minutes a month costs, where prices allow it

Using only published prices, in each vendor's own currency:

- **Hanas:** £49 includes 150 minutes. The other 150 minutes at £0.30 add £45.00, which gives £94.
- **D3x, 20 rooms:** €5 x 20 rooms is €100, plus €100 for 5 phone hours (300 minutes), which gives about €200. This is our reading of two separate lines; confirm the total with D3x.
- **Others:** Dextr AI and Akia publish no price. Motel4, Conduit and HiJiffy publish prices but not the voice minutes per tier in a way that lets us compute 300 minutes.

Prices leave out VAT, setup and overage. Our [cost article](https://gethanas.com/blog/ai-receptionist-for-hotels-what-it-costs-in-the-uk-2026) shows the workings.

## How to choose

![Decision flow: if you need PMS-written bookings, look at Dextr AI, D3x or Akia; if you want a public price and staff approval, look at Hanas or D3x; if you want self-serve setup with no PMS, look at Motel4](/blog/images/dextr-ai-alternatives-small-hotels-dextr-alternatives-choose.png)

_A simple way to narrow the field_

- **You need bookings written into your PMS, and can run a guided rollout:** Dextr AI, D3x or Akia, according to their own claims. Confirm exactly which PMS and which actions.
- **You want a price you can model and a quick start:** D3x, Hanas or Motel4, depending on whether you need PMS connection.
- **You want guest messaging as well as phone:** Conduit or HiJiffy.
- **You want to choose how much the AI decides:** Hanas lets the owner set whether staff approve each booking or it confirms automatically. Others may offer similar controls; not stated on the pages we read.

Whichever you choose, trial it on your own call types. Our post on [how many calls a small hotel misses](https://gethanas.com/blog/how-many-calls-does-a-small-hotel-miss-what-it-costs) shows how to measure first.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Dextr AI suitable for a small independent hotel?

It may be, if you want multi-channel booking into a supported PMS and a guided setup. Its own site lists hotels among many customer types, including large brands in its logos. The homepage shows no price, so ask what you would pay.

### Does Dextr AI publish its pricing?

Not on its homepage, which we read on 17 November 2026. We could not load its pricing page (access error), so we cannot say whether one exists.

### Which alternative has a public price?

D3x, Motel4, Conduit, HiJiffy and Hanas each show prices on their own pages. Akia and Dextr AI did not on the pages we read.

### Do I need PMS integration?

Not necessarily. A request-based or enquiry-capture tool works alongside your PMS, with staff entering bookings. Integration saves staff time but usually means more setup.

### Where is call data stored?

Among these, only HiJiffy (Ireland) and Hanas (transcripts in Ireland, speech processing in the US) state a location on the pages we read. Ask every vendor. For the rules, see [Is it legal to use an AI receptionist at a UK hotel?](https://gethanas.com/blog/is-it-legal-to-use-an-ai-receptionist-at-a-uk-hotel). This is not legal advice.
