A booking request is a guest asking a hotel for a room on given dates. A confirmed booking is the hotel saying yes, usually with a confirmation sent to the guest. Until that yes, the guest has asked and the hotel has not answered. The distinction matters most when an AI receptionist takes the call, because the product's design decides who says yes: the software, or a member of staff.

## The two terms in plain English

**Booking request.** The guest asks for specific dates, a room type and number of guests, and gives contact details. Nothing is held or promised. It may be made by phone, form, email or an AI receptionist.

**Confirmed booking.** The hotel has accepted the request and told the guest. In most hotels that means the booking is entered in the property management system (PMS) or diary, the room is held, and a confirmation is sent, often by email.

## Why the gap exists

Even hotels that answer every call have a gap between asking and confirming. Someone has to check the room is free, that the rate is right for those dates and that any special request can be met. The gap can be seconds, when a system confirms instantly, or hours, when a person approves each one.

An AI receptionist adds one more question: does it only take the request, or can it also say yes? Vendors answer differently. According to its own site (read 20 November 2026), Hanas takes a request and staff approve or decline it, and the site says the AI never confirms a booking. We call that the current default, not a permanent rule. Our post on [whether an AI should confirm hotel bookings](https://gethanas.com/blog/should-an-ai-confirm-hotel-bookings-why-staff-approval-matters) sets out both models and the trade-offs.

## What the guest should be told

The wording on the call and in any follow-up message should match what has actually happened. Compare:

- **A request:** "I have taken your details and passed your request to the team. They will confirm by email or phone. Your room is not confirmed until you hear from them."
- **A confirmation:** "Your booking is confirmed for Friday to Sunday. A confirmation email is on its way."

A voice agent should not use the second kind of sentence unless the system really has created the booking. If you are testing a product, listen for this on your own call types.

## Who says yes: three common set-ups

1. **A person confirms.** The AI or a form collects the request; staff check and reply. Slower, with a human check on rooms and rates.
2. **The booking system confirms.** The request goes into a PMS or booking engine that checks live availability and replies automatically. Fast, but only as accurate as the data.
3. **The AI confirms.** The agent checks availability and creates the booking itself, typically through a PMS connection. Fastest, and it depends most on the connection being reliable.

These are descriptions of categories. We have not tested any other vendor's product, and we cannot say which approach each uses without reading its own pages.

## What this means for small hotels

**Speed against control.** A quick yes can win a caller who would otherwise ring the next hotel. A person-checked yes avoids promising a room that is out of order or already held. We have no measured figure for how often either happens, so we will not invent one.

**Staff response time.** If you use requests, decide how fast you will reply. A request that sits unanswered over a weekend is a lost booking, however careful the process.

**Out-of-hours calls.** A request taken at night waits for the morning. A confirm-on-call model does not, but needs a connected system. See [AI receptionist without a PMS](https://gethanas.com/blog/ai-receptionist-without-a-pms-how-it-works-alongside-your-booking-system) for how requests work alongside an existing system.

## The legal side, briefly

When a booking becomes a binding contract depends on the facts and the law, and we do not give a rule. What we can say is what we read. In our earlier article we read the Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013 on legislation.gov.uk on 17 November 2026. As we read them, a booking made by phone is likely to be a distance contract between a business and a consumer, and three points follow (our interpretation, not advice):

- [Regulation 13](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/13): the trader must give the required information clearly before the consumer is bound.
- [Regulation 16](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/16): the trader must give confirmation of a distance contract on a durable medium, such as an email, within a reasonable time and before service begins.
- [Regulation 28](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/28): the statutory cancellation right does not apply to accommodation for a specific date or period. Your own cancellation terms still need to be clear.

These apply whichever way you confirm. For fuller background, see [Is it legal to use an AI receptionist at a UK hotel?](https://gethanas.com/blog/is-it-legal-to-use-an-ai-receptionist-at-a-uk-hotel) Take advice from a solicitor on your own terms.

## Five questions to ask your vendor or your team

1. Who or what says yes: a person, the PMS or the AI?
2. What exactly does the caller hear at the end of the call?
3. When and how is the confirmation sent, and from which address?
4. What happens to a request nobody answers, and who is alerted?
5. Can I read the transcript to check the wording?

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a booking request a booking?

Not in everyday terms. It is an ask. The hotel's confirmation is what tells the guest they have a room. Whether a binding contract exists earlier is a legal question we do not answer.

### Can an AI receptionist confirm a booking?

Some vendors say their agents can, usually with a PMS connection. Others, including Hanas by default, send a request for staff to approve. Check each vendor's own pages.

### What should a confirmation include?

Regulation 16, as we read it, requires confirmation on a durable medium; what else to include is a question for your own adviser. In practice hotels usually repeat the dates, room type, price and cancellation terms.

### How fast should staff respond to a request?

There is no rule we know of. Decide a target you can meet, and tell guests what to expect.

### Does a request hold the room?

Only if the hotel says so. A clear sentence on the call, such as "we are not holding the room until we confirm", avoids confusion.
