If you run a small independent hotel and are looking at Canary Technologies' AI Voice, the alternatives worth comparing are D3x (PMS-connected, public per-room price), Motel4 (self-serve, no PMS), Dextr AI and Akia (booking-focused, price on request), Conduit and HiJiffy (voice plus guest messaging) and Hanas. The right one depends on four things: whether you want a whole guest-management platform or just the phone answered, whether you want to see a price before talking to sales, how much setup you can take on, and where call data is stored.

## What Canary does well, and who it suits

Fairness first. According to Canary's own site (read 18 November 2026), it is a hospitality platform, not a phone product. Its homepage describes agentic hotel operations and says it is trusted by over 20,000 hotels in 125+ countries (a vendor claim we cannot check). Its product menu covers digital arrivals, guest messaging, upsells, digital tipping, deposits and payments, sales and catering, and an AI Agent Studio.

The AI Voice page describes four assistants: AI Front Desk, AI Central Reservations, AI Concierge and AI Booking. It says AI Voice works with your existing phone system and call-centre software and replaces the menu-tree IVR, and that it syncs with your PMS, CRS, CRM, booking engine and payment systems. The page also shows SOC and PCI DSS v4 certification badges and an award listing from HotelTechAwards for 2026.

If you already want Canary's check-in, messaging and payments tools, adding the phone to the same platform is a real advantage: one vendor, one inbox, one integration.

## Why a small hotel might look elsewhere

These are reasons, not faults. They depend on your hotel.

- **No public price.** The pricing page offers custom pricing. You will need a conversation to find out what a phone-only setup costs.
- **Demo-led buying.** The main call to action on the pages we read is "Book a Demo". A one-person B&B may prefer something it can read about and start alone.
- **Breadth.** If you only need calls answered, a platform with dozens of modules may be more than you need.
- **Details not stated.** The AI Voice page does not say how many languages it speaks, where call data is stored, which PMSs are supported by name for voice, or whether a card payment is taken during a call.

## The alternatives, one by one

### D3x: PMS-connected, with a public per-room price

D3x publishes an "Independent & SMB" tier at €5 per room a month for messaging, with phone AI as an add-on at €100 a month for 5 hours, then €12 per extra hour. Its [AI hotel receptionist page](https://www.d3x.ai/solutions/ai-hotel-receptionist) says the phone AI can look up and confirm changes to reservations in your PMS and names Mews, Oracle Opera, Cloudbeds and Apaleo. Whether it creates new bookings, and where data is stored, are not stated on the pages we read (17 November 2026). See [Hanas vs D3x](https://gethanas.com/compare/d3x).

### Motel4: self-serve, no PMS needed

Motel4's site describes a three-step setup of about 15 minutes: add property details, forward the front-desk line, go live. It says no PMS integration or new hardware is needed and that it captures booking enquiries with the caller's number. Pricing is a free 30 minutes a month, then $149, $299 and $599 a month, with a 30-day money-back guarantee. The minutes in each paid tier, and data location, are not stated.

### Dextr AI and Akia: booking-focused, price on request

Dextr AI says it books across calls, texts and web chat, names several PMSs and uses a seven-day guided setup; its homepage shows no price. Akia's pricing page lists Basic, Pro and Max, each with a "Talk to Sales" button, and puts AI Voice, call transfers and call summaries under Max only. Its homepage (18 November 2026) says the voice agent holds a conversation and takes payment before hanging up. Treat both as vendor claims and ask for a demo with your own PMS.

### Hanas: public price, staff approval, early access

Hanas answers the phone at any hour in the caller's language, says it is an AI, quotes only the rates the hotel enters, and sends a booking request to staff to approve or decline. Setup starts from your website: it drafts a profile, and your team reviews it before anything goes live.

The price is on the page: £49 a month with 150 minutes, then £0.30 a minute (early-access price, excluding VAT, which the site says may change before general availability). The demo offers English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Russian and Arabic.

**Where Hanas is the weaker choice.** It has no PMS integration yet, so staff enter approved bookings themselves (Mews, Cloudbeds, Little Hotelier, Guestline and eviivo are on the roadmap, with no dates). By default it sends requests rather than confirming bookings, and by default it does not take payment. That is the current default and may change, but if you need either today, Canary, Dextr AI, D3x or Akia are better matches on their own claims. It is also a phone product only: no check-in, messaging or payments modules. Hanas is in early access with no customer reviews or case studies yet. See the [privacy notice](https://gethanas.com/privacy) and [Hanas for boutique hotels](https://gethanas.com/for/boutique-hotels).

## What 300 minutes a month costs, where prices allow it

Using only published prices, in each vendor's own currency:

- **Hanas:** £49 includes 150 minutes. The other 150 minutes at £0.30 add £45.00, which gives £94.
- **D3x, 20 rooms:** €5 x 20 rooms is €100, plus €100 for 5 phone hours (300 minutes), which gives about €200. This is our reading of two separate lines; confirm the total with D3x.
- **Canary, Dextr AI, Akia:** no public price, so no total.

Prices leave out VAT, setup and overage. Our [cost article](https://gethanas.com/blog/ai-receptionist-for-hotels-what-it-costs-in-the-uk-2026) shows the workings.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Canary suitable for a small independent hotel?

It may be. Canary lists "Independent Hotels" as a property type and describes products for hotels of many sizes. Pricing is custom, so ask what you would pay for AI Voice alone.

### Does Canary publish its pricing?

No. Its pricing page, read on 18 November 2026, offers custom pricing.

### Does Canary's AI Voice take card payments on the call?

Its voice page says it syncs with payment systems but does not say whether a card is taken during a call. Ask.

### Do I need PMS integration?

Not necessarily. A request-based or enquiry-capture tool works alongside your PMS, with staff entering bookings. Integration saves staff time but usually means more setup.

### Where is call data stored?

None of the Canary pages we read state a location. Among the others, only HiJiffy (Ireland) and Hanas (transcripts in Ireland, speech processing in the US) state one. Ask every vendor.
