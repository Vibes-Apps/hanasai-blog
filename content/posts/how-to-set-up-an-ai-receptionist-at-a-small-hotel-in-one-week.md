A small hotel can get an AI receptionist ready in about a week if most of the time goes on decisions rather than software: which calls it handles, what it may say, and what your team does with the result. This plan spreads those decisions over seven days. It is a general plan that works for any vendor; the timings are our suggestion, not a promise from any product.

## Day 1: Decide what you want it to do

Write one page, in plain words, answering four questions:

1. **When should it answer?** Overnight only, busy periods only, or every call. Our [out-of-hours checklist](https://gethanas.com/blog/out-of-hours-call-handling-checklist-for-small-hotels) helps you define those windows.
2. **Which channels?** Phone is the usual starting point. Some products also handle messages or other channels; start with one so you can judge it.
3. **What may it do?** Answer questions, quote rates you have entered, take details for a booking request. Decide whether a booking is confirmed by a person or automatically, and make that a deliberate choice. Our guide to [booking requests versus confirmed bookings](https://gethanas.com/blog/booking-request-vs-confirmed-booking-what-is-the-difference) explains the difference in wording.
4. **What must always go to a person?** Complaints, safety worries, group bookings, anything unusual.

## Day 2: Gather the facts the AI will repeat

An AI receptionist is only as accurate as what it is given. Collect:

- Room types, maximum guests per room and your current rates by date.
- Check-in and check-out times, breakfast hours, parking, pets, accessibility.
- Cancellation and deposit policy, worded exactly as it appears on your website.
- Phone number and email for requests, and who reads them.

If your website already holds most of this, good: some tools draft a profile from your website. According to its own site (read 24 November 2026), Hanas does this and asks your team to review the profile before anything goes live, flagging missing details instead of guessing. Whichever product you use, read the draft line by line. A wrong check-in time repeated to fifty callers is worse than a missed call.

## Day 3: Configure and review

Enter or import the rates, then review the profile. Pay attention to:

- **Rates and dates.** Does a date range overlap or leave a gap? What should happen for dates with no rate?
- **Policies.** Is each one stated the way you would say it?
- **The opening line.** Callers should hear that they are speaking to an AI, and if calls are recorded, that too. See our guide to the [recording notice](https://gethanas.com/blog/call-recording-notice-for-hotels-what-to-say-at-the-start-of-a-call) and our [post on greetings](https://gethanas.com/blog/hotel-phone-greeting-what-to-say-and-why-an-ai-should-say-it-is-an-ai).
- **Languages.** If you get foreign-language callers, check which languages are supported. See [how to handle foreign-language callers](https://gethanas.com/blog/how-to-handle-foreign-language-callers-at-a-small-uk-hotel).

## Day 4: Test with real questions

Ring the number yourself, then ask a colleague who did not set it up to ring. Use a script of real questions from your own call history:

- "Do you have a double for next Friday for two nights?"
- "Is breakfast included, and do you do gluten-free?"
- "Can I bring my dog?"
- "I booked online and need to change the date."
- "I'm locked out of my room." (This one should reach a person.)
- A question the AI has no answer for, to see what it does.

Score each call: right answer, wrong answer, or handed over correctly. Fix the profile and test again. We have not run this test on any particular product for this article, so treat the list as a starting point.

## Day 5: Brief the team

The software side is the easy half. The people side decides whether bookings are won:

- **What arrives:** an email, a dashboard entry or both. Show everyone an example.
- **Who owns it:** a named person for each shift, with a reply deadline you can meet.
- **How to reply:** in the caller's language where possible, by phone or email.
- **What to do with a mistake:** a simple way to flag a wrong answer so the profile is fixed.

Tell staff plainly that the aim is to free them from the phone during busy moments, not to replace them.

## Day 6: Go live in a limited way

Start with the lowest-risk window, for example overnight only. Keep a person reachable for urgent in-house problems, as in your [out-of-hours plan](https://gethanas.com/blog/out-of-hours-call-handling-checklist-for-small-hotels). Check the first calls the next morning rather than waiting for the end of the week.

## Day 7: Review and adjust

Read or listen to every call from the first days. Note:

- Calls answered correctly, wrongly or handed over.
- Questions that came up that the profile did not cover.
- How long your team took to reply to requests.
- Anything a guest said about the experience.

Adjust the profile, then widen the hours or the scope only if the results justify it. Our post on [how many calls a small hotel misses](https://gethanas.com/blog/how-many-calls-does-a-small-hotel-miss-what-it-costs) shows how to count your own baseline so you can compare.

## What about your booking system?

Check whether the product connects to your property management system (PMS). Many small-hotel tools start with email and a dashboard and add integrations later. On its site (read 24 November 2026) Hanas lists email, a web dashboard and spreadsheet rate import as working today and PMS integrations as on its roadmap, with staff entering approved bookings as they do now. Our [guide to working without a PMS integration](https://gethanas.com/blog/ai-receptionist-without-a-pms-how-it-works-alongside-your-booking-system) covers that set-up.

## Setup checklist

1. One-page scope: hours, channels, what it may do, what goes to a person.
2. Rates, policies and times collected and checked.
3. Profile reviewed line by line.
4. Opening line says it is an AI, and says if calls are recorded.
5. Test calls scored and fixed.
6. Team briefed, owner named for each shift.
7. Limited go-live with a human fallback.
8. Review of the first calls, then a decision on widening.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a hotel really set this up in a week?

Often the configuration is the quick part. The week is mostly for checking your information, testing and briefing staff. If your rates and policies are messy, allow longer.

### Do I need a technical person?

Not necessarily. Tools for small hotels aim to avoid IT projects, but someone has to own the content and the follow-up.

### What if the AI gets something wrong?

Fix the profile, test again and keep a person as the fallback for anything unusual. This is why we suggest starting with a limited window.

### Should I tell guests it is an AI?

We recommend saying so at the start of every call. See our [greeting guide](https://gethanas.com/blog/hotel-phone-greeting-what-to-say-and-why-an-ai-should-say-it-is-an-ai) for wording.
