A hotel channel manager is software that keeps your room availability and rates the same on every website that sells your rooms. When a room sells on one site, the channel manager updates the others, so you do not sell the same room twice. Small hotels that sell on only one or two sites often do not need one. Hotels that sell on several online travel agents (OTAs) usually do. This guide explains what it does, how it differs from a booking engine and a PMS, and what to ask a vendor. It is general information, and we do not quote prices or compare products.

## What a channel manager does

A "channel" is anywhere a guest can book your hotel: your own website, an OTA, a phone call, a walk-in. Online channels each keep their own copy of your availability and rates. Without a link between them, you would have to log in to each one and change it by hand every time a room sells.

A channel manager sits in the middle. You set availability and rates once. It sends them out to each connected site, and when a booking arrives on any of them, it takes the booking in and reduces availability everywhere else.

![Flow: a booking arrives from an online travel agent, the channel manager updates availability on every other channel, the booking reaches the PMS](/blog/images/cm-flow.png)

_How a channel manager keeps channels in step_

In plain terms, a channel manager usually covers:

- **Availability sync:** closing or reopening rooms on every connected site.
- **Rate distribution:** sending your rates, minimum stays and restrictions to each channel.
- **Booking import:** pulling bookings from the channels into one place.
- **A link to your PMS:** so the booking lands where your team works.

What it can do varies by product and by which channels it supports, so ask for the list of connections, not just the headline.

## How it differs from a booking engine and a PMS

The three are often sold together and often confused. The short version: the **booking engine** sells on your own website, the **channel manager** keeps other sites in step, and the **PMS** holds the reservations and runs the day-to-day. Our guide to [booking engine vs channel manager vs PMS](https://gethanas.com/blog/booking-engine-vs-channel-manager-vs-pms-what-is-the-difference) goes through each, our [booking engine guide](https://gethanas.com/blog/what-is-a-hotel-booking-engine-a-uk-guide-for-small-hotels) covers the direct-booking side, and our [PMS guide](https://gethanas.com/blog/what-is-a-property-management-system-pms-a-guide-for-small-hotels) covers the system behind them.

| Tool | Main job | Who it faces |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Booking engine** | Takes bookings on your own website | Guests |
| **Channel manager** | Keeps availability and rates the same across sites | OTAs and other sales channels |
| **PMS** | Holds reservations, check-in, rooms, guest records | Your team |

Bundles are common. Some PMS products include a channel manager, and some channel managers include a basic booking engine. The label matters less than which job is covered and who maintains the connection.

## Do you need one?

There is no fixed rule. These questions help.

![Four icons for deciding on a channel manager: number of channels, how often rooms sell out, who updates by hand, PMS link](/blog/images/cm-decision.png)

_Four things that decide whether you need a channel manager_

1. **How many sites sell your rooms?** With one or two, updating by hand can work. With three or more, the risk of a missed update grows.
2. **How often do you sell out?** If rooms rarely fill, a double booking is less likely. If weekends sell out, it matters more.
3. **Who updates the sites, and when?** If it depends on one person remembering after every booking, that is the weak point.
4. **Does your PMS already include one?** Check before buying a separate tool.

A channel manager does not remove the need to check. Sync can fail or lag, so look at your channels now and then, especially after a rate change.

## What a small hotel should check before choosing

![Table card of six things to check in a channel manager: channels, PMS connection, update speed, rates and rules, support, cost and contract](/blog/images/cm-checklist.png)

_Six things to check before you choose_

| Area | Question to ask |
| --- | --- |
| **Channels supported** | Does it connect to every site you sell on today? Is each connection included or an extra? |
| **PMS connection** | Does it send bookings into your PMS and read availability back? Who maintains it? |
| **Speed of updates** | How quickly does a change reach each channel, and what happens if an update fails? |
| **Rates and rules** | Can it send your rate types, minimum stays and closed dates? |
| **Support** | Who do you call when a channel stops syncing, and when are they available? |
| **Cost and contract** | What is the total over a year, including set-up, channel connections and any per-booking charge? |

### Cost

Vendors charge in different ways: a monthly subscription, a fee per room or per connected channel, a percentage of bookings, or a bundle inside a PMS. We have not compared prices and do not quote any, because they change and depend on your size. Ask for the total cost over a year, and what happens if you leave.

## Where phone and messages fit

A channel manager covers online channels only. Guests who ring, message on WhatsApp or Instagram, or start on a booking site and then call with a question are outside it. If those enquiries are handled by hand, they are the ones most likely to be missed when the team is busy or the hotel is closed.

## A simple way to decide

1. List every channel that sells your rooms today.
2. Check what your PMS already offers.
3. Count how many manual updates you make after a booking, and how often one is late.
4. If you shortlist a product, ask the questions in the table above and get the answers in writing.
5. Test it with a real booking and a rate change, then check every channel.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a channel manager the same as a booking engine?

No. A booking engine takes bookings on your own website. A channel manager distributes availability and rates to other channels such as OTAs. Some products offer both.

### Does a channel manager stop double bookings?

It reduces the risk by updating every channel after a booking, but it cannot guarantee it. Updates can lag or fail, and bookings by phone or in person still need to reach your PMS.

### Does a small hotel need a channel manager?

Not always. If you sell on one or two sites and rooms rarely sell out, manual updates may be enough. It matters more as the number of channels grows.

### Do I still need a PMS?

Yes, in most cases. A channel manager moves availability and bookings between sites; the PMS is where reservations and guest records are kept and your team works.

### How much does a channel manager cost?

It depends on the vendor, the number of rooms and channels, and whether it is bundled. Ask for a yearly total in writing.
