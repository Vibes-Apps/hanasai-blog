## How many calls does a small hotel miss?

We could not find a reliable published figure for how many calls small hotels miss. What the best available data does show is that the phone is still a major way guests book direct: HOTREC's 2026 European study puts phone at 14.1% of overnight stays in its 2025 data. You can measure your own missed calls in a week, and a simple formula turns that count into revenue at risk.

## What the data says about phone bookings

The best primary source on how European hotels get their bookings is the HOTREC European Hotel Distribution Study, from the association of European hotel and restaurant trade bodies. The 2026 edition was published in September 2026 and covers 2025 distribution. We read the full study and the 2024 edition. The tables report the share of overnight stays by channel, using weighted values that exclude aggregated data from chains.

| Channel (share of overnight stays) | 2023 (2024 study) | 2025 (2026 study) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phone | 15.4% | 14.1% |
| Email | 13.2% | 13.5% |
| Booking on own website with availability check | 12.3% | 13.9% |
| Contact form on own website | 4.9% | 5.2% |
| Walk-in | 3.7% | 3.0% |
| Mail or fax | 1.4% | 1.6% |
| All direct channels | 50.9% | 51.3% |
| Online travel agencies (OTAs) | 29.6% | 29.9% |

Source: HOTREC, [European Hotel Distribution Study 2026](https://www.hotrec.eu/media/static/files/import/all_news_2026_2026_35/full-study_european-hotel-distribution-study-2026_hotrec.pdf), weighted values 2025; and [European Hotel Distribution Study 2024](https://www.hotrec.eu/media/static/files/import/all_news_2024_2024_21/hotrec-distribution-study-2024.pdf), weighted values 2023.

The study offers three points that matter for a small hotel:

- **Phone is the largest single direct channel in both editions, narrowly in 2025.** In 2025, 14.1% of overnight stays came by phone, against 13.9% for booking on the hotel's own website and 13.5% for email. In 2023 the lead was wider: 15.4% against 13.2% for email. Direct channels as a whole accounted for 51.3% in 2025.
- **The study says small independents lean on direct channels.** Its [executive summary](https://www.hotrec.eu/media/static/files/import/all_news_2026_2026_35/executive-summary_european-hotel-distribution-study-2026_hotrec.pdf) says the strongest direct booking performance is among small, independent hotels and properties in rural, alpine and small-town destinations.
- **Direct is shifting online.** The study's executive summary (page 5 of the 2026 study) says direct distribution is moving from traditional contact channels towards real-time booking engines. The table shows own-website booking rising from 12.3% to 13.9% between the two editions, while phone slipped from 15.4% to 14.1% (different samples, so treat the change as indicative).

**Limits you should know.** These are survey-based estimates, and the two editions use different samples, so small moves between editions may be noise. The country tables we read do not name the UK separately, and we could not confirm how UK hotels are covered. The study measures how bookings arrive, not how many calls go unanswered, and it does not show whether a missed call turned into a booking by email or another route.

## What we could not find

We looked for an independent study of how many hotel calls go unanswered, and how many callers who reach voicemail try elsewhere. We searched the web and the websites of UK and European hospitality bodies in November 2026 and did not find a credible one.

The figures we found were published by vendors that sell call answering or AI phone products, and they were not about hotels. We would not base a decision on them, and we do not use them here. If you see a precise missed-call percentage for hotels without a named study, a sample size and a year, treat it as unsupported.

## Measure your own missed calls in a week

You do not need a benchmark if you count your own calls. Do this for seven days, including a weekend.

1. **Pull the call log.** Your phone provider's itemised bill or portal, or your phone system's call history, shows missed and unanswered inbound calls with the time and the caller's number.
2. **Mark when they were missed.** Note whether each missed call came overnight, at the weekend, during check-in or breakfast, or at another busy time.
3. **Sort out the noise.** Remove numbers you recognise as suppliers, sales calls and repeat callers.
4. **Call back a sample.** Ring five or ten unknown numbers the next working day, keep it brief and courteous, and ask what they wanted. Skip numbers you recognise as sales or spam. Note how many were booking enquiries and how many had already booked elsewhere. This is the most useful number you will collect.
5. **Check voicemail.** Compare the voicemails you received against the missed calls. If few callers leave a message, most of your missed calls are invisible to you.

A simple log you can copy:

| Date | Time | Missed? | Number | Voicemail left? | Called back? | Booking enquiry? | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |

## The formula, with example numbers

**Revenue at risk = missed booking enquiries × share who would have booked × average booking value**

The first number comes from your log. The second comes from your call-back sample. For the third, use your own average. As one reference point, [VisitBritain reports an average daily rate of £186](https://www.visitbritain.org/research-insights/england-hotel-occupancy-latest) for England hotels in September 2026 (Amadeus data, a series VisitBritain says is in development). That is an England-wide figure, so use your own rate if you know it.

The table below uses an example booking of £330, which is two nights at a round £165 a night. £165 is an example rate, not a market figure. **These are example inputs, not findings.** Your numbers will differ, and none of these is a prediction of what any hotel loses.

| Missed booking enquiries a month | 10% would have booked | 25% would have booked | 40% would have booked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | £330 | £825 | £1,320 |
| 20 | £660 | £1,650 | £2,640 |
| 40 | £1,320 | £3,300 | £5,280 |

**Read this as revenue, not profit,** and as an illustrative range, not a forecast. Some callers will ring back, email or book online instead. Each booking also carries costs.

## Ways to reduce missed calls

Each option has trade-offs, and the right one depends on what your log shows.

- **Forward calls to a mobile out of hours.** Low cost, though forwarding can carry call charges, and it depends on someone being available and willing at all hours.
- **A clear voicemail greeting and a fast call-back habit.** Low cost. It works only if callers leave messages and you call back quickly.
- **A human answering service.** Real people take messages. Some UK providers publish their prices, and we compare them with AI options and night staff in [what an AI receptionist costs in the UK](https://gethanas.com/blog/ai-receptionist-hotel-cost-published-prices/).
- **An AI phone receptionist.** In principle it can answer every call and take details. Products differ on whether they confirm bookings or send a request to staff, on PMS connection and on price. Our [comparison of seven options for small UK hotels](https://gethanas.com/blog/best-ai-receptionist-small-hotels-uk/) covers this.
- **Check the legal side first.** Recording calls and using an AI vendor bring data protection duties, which we summarise in [Is it legal to use an AI receptionist at a UK hotel?](https://gethanas.com/blog/ai-receptionist-uk-hotel-legal/)
- **Online booking and chat.** They shift demand away from the phone, but HOTREC's data shows phone is still a major direct channel, so they do not remove the need to answer it.

## Frequently asked questions

### What share of hotel bookings come by phone?

About one in seven overnight stays in HOTREC's 2025 data, with an earlier edition showing slightly more. The UK is not broken out separately, and the study measures how bookings arrive, not whether your own phone is answered.

### How many missed calls is normal for a small hotel?

We found no credible benchmark, so we cannot give a normal figure. Count your own for a week using the method above.

### Do callers leave voicemails?

We found no independent hotel figure. You can estimate it yourself by comparing the voicemails you receive with the missed calls in your log.

### Does a missed call always mean a lost booking?

No. Some callers email or call back. That is why the table above shows a range of conversion rates and why we call the result revenue at risk.
