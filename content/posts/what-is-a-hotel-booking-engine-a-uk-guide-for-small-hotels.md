A hotel booking engine is the software on your own website that lets guests check availability, pick a room and rate, and book without phoning or emailing you. It is your direct sales channel. For a small hotel, the useful question is not "which engine is best" but "what do I need it to do, and what does it need to connect to". This guide explains the parts, gives a checklist of questions for vendors, and covers two UK rules that affect how an engine shows prices and cancellations. It is general information, not legal advice.

## What a booking engine does

When a guest clicks "Book now" on your website, the booking engine is the screen that follows. It asks for dates and the number of guests, shows the rooms that are free, shows the rate for each, collects guest details, and confirms the booking. Behind that screen it has to know what is available right now, which is why it needs a link to your room inventory.

![Flow: guest picks dates on the hotel website, the booking engine checks availability, the guest pays or guarantees the stay, the booking reaches the PMS](/blog/images/booking-be-flow.png)

_What happens when a guest books through a booking engine_

In plain terms, a booking engine usually covers:

- **Availability and rates:** it shows what you have for the dates searched.
- **The booking form:** guest details, room choice, any extras you offer.
- **Payment or guarantee:** many take a deposit, full payment or a card guarantee. How this works depends on the vendor and the payment provider they use.
- **Confirmation messages:** an email to the guest, and a notice to you.
- **A record of the booking:** ideally sent straight into your PMS, otherwise someone has to copy it across.

## How it differs from a channel manager and a PMS

These three are often confused. The short version: the booking engine sells on your website, the channel manager keeps your availability and rates in step across online travel agents, and the PMS holds the reservations and runs operations. Our guide to [booking engine vs channel manager vs PMS](https://gethanas.com/blog/booking-engine-vs-channel-manager-vs-pms-what-is-the-difference) goes through each, and our [PMS guide](https://gethanas.com/blog/what-is-a-property-management-system-pms-a-guide-for-small-hotels) covers the system behind them.

Two practical points follow from this:

1. **A booking engine on its own does not stop double bookings.** If it cannot see bookings that came in by phone, email or an online travel agent, it may sell a room you no longer have. The link to the PMS or channel manager is what keeps one stock of rooms.
2. **Bundles are common.** Some PMS products include a booking engine; some booking engines include a basic channel manager. The label matters less than which job is covered and who maintains it.

## What a small hotel should look for

There is no single right set of features. A five-room guest house and a 40-room inn need different things. These are the areas worth checking, with questions to put to a vendor.

### Cost

Vendors charge in different ways: a monthly subscription, a commission on each booking, a fee bundled into a PMS, or a mix. We have not compared prices in this guide and do not quote any, because they change and depend on the vendor and your size. When you ask for a quote, ask for the total cost over a year, including set-up, payment processing and any per-booking charge.

## Two UK rules worth knowing

The rules below apply to selling to consumers in the UK. They are summarised from the official sources listed at the end. How they apply to your hotel is a question for a qualified adviser.

### 1. Show the full price at the start

The Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 brought in a ban on what is sometimes called "drip pricing". The CMA's guidance on unfair commercial practices says that showing consumers an initial headline price and then adding mandatory charges as they go through the purchase is prohibited, and that these provisions apply to practices from 6 April 2025.

For a booking engine, the practical question is what the first price on screen includes. If a charge is unavoidable for the guest, check with your adviser whether it belongs in the price shown at the start rather than appearing on a later step. Genuinely optional extras are treated differently from mandatory charges; the CMA guidance explains where the line sits.

### 2. Cancellation rights are different for accommodation

Many online purchases come with a statutory right to cancel within a period. Under the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, regulation 28 lists exceptions, and one of them is the supply of accommodation if the contract provides for a specific date or period of performance. So a booking for set dates does not automatically carry that statutory cancellation right.

That does not mean you can be vague. Your own cancellation terms still need to be clear to the guest before they book, and the booking engine should show them in the booking flow. Consumer protection law on unfair terms and misleading practices still applies, so have your terms reviewed.

## Where a phone and messaging fit

Not every guest books online. Some ring, some send a message on Instagram or WhatsApp, and some start on the website and then call with a question. A booking engine covers the website only. ## A simple way to decide

1. List how guests book today: website, phone, messages, online travel agents.
2. Check what your PMS already offers. You may have a booking engine included.
3. Shortlist two or three engines and run a test booking on each, on a phone.
4. Ask the vendor questions in the table above and get the answers in writing.
5. Read your own booking flow as a guest would: first price, cancellation terms, confirmation email.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a booking engine if I already have a website?

A website alone cannot take a live booking. You need either a booking engine, a link out to one, or a way to take enquiries by form, phone or email. Many small hotels use a booking engine so that guests can book at any hour.

### Is a booking engine the same as a channel manager?

No. A booking engine sells on your own site; a channel manager distributes availability and rates to other channels such as online travel agents. Some products offer both.

### Does a booking engine take payment?

Some do, through a payment provider, and some only take a card guarantee or no payment at all. It varies by vendor and set-up, so ask.

### Do guests have a legal right to cancel an online hotel booking?

For accommodation booked for a specific date or period, the statutory cancellation right in the Consumer Contracts Regulations does not generally apply, because regulation 28 lists it as an exception. Your own terms and wider consumer law still apply, so check them with an adviser.

### Can a booking engine show the price without taxes and fees?

Under the CMA's guidance, a headline price followed by mandatory charges added later is prohibited drip pricing. Ask your adviser how this applies to every charge a guest must pay.
