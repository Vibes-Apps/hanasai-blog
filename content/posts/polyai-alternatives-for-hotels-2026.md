If you run a small hotel and are looking at PolyAI for phone answering, the alternatives worth comparing are D3x (PMS-connected, public per-room price), Dextr AI (booking across channels), Akia and Canary Technologies (phone bundled into a wider guest platform), Motel4 (self-serve, no PMS) and Hanas (public price, staff approval). PolyAI describes itself as an enterprise platform and publishes no platform price, so the real question is whether you want to buy through a sales conversation or from a price you can read first.

## What PolyAI does well, and who it suits

Fairness first. According to PolyAI's own pages (read 19 November 2026), it builds voice agents for large organisations. The homepage names customers such as NatWest, Metro Bank, PG&E, Foot Locker and Golden Nugget, and claims 90% of calls are resolved end to end on its largest deployments. Those are PolyAI's own figures, and we have not checked them.

Its hotels page lists taking room reservations, housekeeping requests and billing questions, and says the agent can offer alternative rooms and process payments over the phone. On security, the homepage lists SOC 2+ Type 2, ISO 27001, PCI DSS and GDPR, and says data residency is available in the US, UK, EU and APAC with no cross-region transfer. That is a lot of assurance in one place.

If you run a group or a large property with a contact-centre-sized call volume, a platform like this is built for you.

## Why a small hotel might look elsewhere

These are reasons, not faults. They depend on your hotel.

- **No platform price.** The pricing page says ongoing use is "priced on a per-minute basis" and offers a "Talk to our team" form. A separate API tier shows 2¢ per connected minute, but telephony and orchestration are "not included", so it is not a like-for-like number for a hotel phone line.
- **Enterprise positioning.** We found no mention of small or independent hotels on the pages we read.
- **Setup not described.** Implementation steps and timescale are not stated. The hotels page says "No need to change your tech stack".
- **No named PMS.** The hotels page says it integrates with PMSs but lists none. Ask which one, and what the agent can read and write.
- **Languages not listed.** The homepage says "Multilingual: By design" without naming languages.

## The alternatives, one by one

### D3x: PMS-connected, with a public per-room price

D3x publishes an "Independent & SMB" tier at €5 per room a month for messaging, with phone AI as an add-on at €100 a month for 5 hours, then €12 per extra hour. Its [AI hotel receptionist page](https://www.d3x.ai/solutions/ai-hotel-receptionist) says the phone AI can look up and confirm changes to reservations in your PMS. Whether it creates new bookings, and where data is stored, are not stated on the pages we read. See [Hanas vs D3x](https://gethanas.com/compare/d3x).

### Dextr AI: booking across calls, texts and chat

Dextr AI says it books across calls, texts and web chat, names several PMSs and uses a seven-day guided setup. Its homepage shows no price. More in our [Dextr AI alternatives article](https://gethanas.com/blog/dextr-ai-alternatives-for-small-independent-hotels-2026).

### Akia and Canary: the phone inside a wider platform

Both sell AI Voice as part of a larger guest-communication offer and neither shows a price. See our [Akia alternatives](https://gethanas.com/blog/akia-alternatives-for-small-hotels-2026) and [Canary alternatives](https://gethanas.com/blog/canary-technologies-alternatives-for-independent-hotels-2026) articles.

### Motel4: self-serve, no PMS needed

Motel4's site describes a three-step setup of about 15 minutes: add property details, forward the front-desk line, go live. It says no PMS integration or new hardware is needed. Pricing is a free 30 minutes a month, then $149, $299 and $599 a month. Minutes in each paid tier and data location are not stated.

### Hanas: public price, staff approval, early access

Hanas answers the phone at any hour in the caller's language, says it is an AI, quotes only the rates the hotel enters, and sends a booking request to staff to approve or decline. Setup starts from your website link: it drafts a profile of rooms, hours and policies, and your team reviews it before anything goes live.

The prices are on the page, all excluding VAT and subject to change before general availability: Starter £49 a month with 150 minutes then £0.30 a minute; Pro £99 with 400 minutes then £0.25; Premium £199 with 1,000 minutes then £0.20. A 14-day or 60-minute free trial runs on your own number, whichever comes first.

**Where Hanas is the weaker choice.** It has no PMS integration yet, so staff enter approved bookings themselves (Mews, Cloudbeds, Little Hotelier, Guestline and eviivo are on the roadmap, with no dates). By default it sends requests rather than confirming bookings, and by default it does not take payment. That is the current default and may change, but if you need either today, PolyAI, Akia, Dextr AI or Canary are better matches on their own claims. It is a phone product only. It is in early access with no customer reviews or case studies yet, and it holds no certifications we can point to. See the [privacy notice](https://gethanas.com/privacy).

## What 300 minutes a month costs, where prices allow it

Using only published prices, in each vendor's own currency:

- **Hanas Starter:** £49 includes 150 minutes. The other 150 minutes at £0.30 add £45.00, which gives £94.
- **Hanas Pro:** £99 includes 400 minutes, so 300 minutes costs £99. Starter is £5 cheaper at this volume.
- **D3x, 20 rooms:** €5 x 20 rooms is €100, plus €100 for 5 phone hours (300 minutes), which gives about €200. This is our reading of two separate lines; confirm the total with D3x.
- **PolyAI, Akia, Canary, Dextr AI:** no public platform price, so no total.

Prices leave out VAT, setup and overage. Our [cost article](https://gethanas.com/blog/ai-receptionist-for-hotels-what-it-costs-in-the-uk-2026) shows the workings.

## How to choose

![Decision flow: for enterprise scale and contact-centre volume look at PolyAI; for PMS-written bookings look at Dextr AI, D3x, Akia or Canary; for a public price and staff approval look at Hanas or D3x; for self-serve setup with no PMS look at Motel4](/blog/images/polyai-alternatives-hotels-polyai-alternatives-choose.png)

_A simple way to narrow the field_

- **You have group-level volume and a procurement process:** PolyAI is built for that, on its own description. Ask for a quote and a named PMS list.
- **You need bookings written into your PMS:** Dextr AI, D3x, Akia or Canary, according to their own claims. Confirm which PMS and which actions.
- **You want a price you can model and a quick start:** D3x, Hanas or Motel4, depending on whether you need PMS connection.
- **You want a person to approve every booking:** Hanas does this by default. Others may offer it; not stated on the pages we read.

Whichever you choose, trial it on your own call types. Our post on [how many calls a small hotel misses](https://gethanas.com/blog/how-many-calls-does-a-small-hotel-miss-what-it-costs) shows how to measure first.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does PolyAI publish its pricing?

Partly. Its pricing page says voice agent use is priced per minute but shows no platform rate, and lists a separate API tier at 2¢ per connected minute without telephony. Page read on 19 November 2026.

### Is PolyAI aimed at small hotels?

Its homepage and pricing page position it as an enterprise platform, with "Book a demo" as the main call to action. Small-hotel suitability is not stated either way.

### Does PolyAI take payment on the call?

Its hotels page says it can process payments over the phone. We have not tested this and the page does not say how card details are handled. Ask for a demo with your payment setup.

### Do I need PMS integration?

Not necessarily. A request-based tool works alongside your PMS, with staff entering bookings. Integration saves staff time but usually means more setup.

### Where is call data stored?

PolyAI's homepage lists US, UK, EU and APAC residency options. For other vendors it is mostly not stated. Ask every vendor. For the rules, see [Is it legal to use an AI receptionist at a UK hotel?](https://gethanas.com/blog/is-it-legal-to-use-an-ai-receptionist-at-a-uk-hotel). This is not legal advice.
