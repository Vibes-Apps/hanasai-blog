Guests who ring on the way to a hotel usually ask about five things: where to park, whether they can check in early, how to get in if the desk is unstaffed, how to find the place, and whether anything has changed since they booked. These calls arrive when the front desk is busiest, so a simple set of ready answers, and a plan for who picks up, makes arrival smoother for guests and staff.

## Why check-in time is the hard time for the phone

Many small hotels and B&Bs open check-in in the afternoon. That is also when guests start ringing: they are on the motorway, at the station or outside a locked door. The person who would answer is often the same person checking in the guest standing at the desk.

You cannot count the cost of those calls from this article, but you can count them in your own building. Our post on [how to count your own missed calls](https://gethanas.com/blog/how-many-calls-does-a-small-hotel-miss-what-it-costs) shows a simple method. Look at the hour before and after your check-in start time first.

## The five questions guests ask on the way

### 1. "Where do I park?"

State the facts: is there on-site parking, how many spaces, is it free or charged, and is it first come first served. If parking is nearby but not yours, give the street or car park name and say it is not managed by you. Do not promise a space you cannot guarantee.

> Example wording: "We have six spaces behind the building, free for guests, first come first served. If they are full, the nearest public car park is on the High Street, about a three-minute walk."

### 2. "Can I check in early?"

Decide your policy once. A typical structure has three answers:

- **Yes, the room is ready.** Say so and give the door or entry details.
- **Not yet.** Say when the room is expected to be ready, and offer to store luggage if you can.
- **We will try.** Take a mobile number and say you will message when the room is ready, only if someone will actually do that.

Early check-in is often a matter of housekeeping timing, so the answer can change by the hour. Make it clear who decides, and avoid a flat promise.

### 3. "How do I get in? Nobody is at the desk."

If your reception is unstaffed at times, guests need a clear fallback. Cover:

- Whether there is a key safe or entry code, and how guests receive it.
- A number they can ring for help on arrival.
- What to do if the code does not work.

Because this is an access and safety matter, an out-of-hours or locked-out call should reach a person if at all possible. See our [out-of-hours call handling checklist](https://gethanas.com/blog/out-of-hours-call-handling-checklist-for-small-hotels) for how to set the escalation rules.

### 4. "How do I find you?"

Give the last mile in plain language, not just the postcode. Mention the landmark, the turning and the sign. If satellite navigation is known to misdirect guests to the wrong street, say so. Keep a short, tested script for each common starting point, such as the station or the main road.

### 5. "I booked, but something has changed."

This covers late arrival, a different number of guests, a change of dates or a request such as a cot. These are not all the same:

- **Late arrival.** Note the expected time and tell the guest how late entry works.
- **Changes to the booking.** Take the details and say who will confirm and by when, or make the change if you or your system can do so there and then.
- **Special requests.** Record them. Do not promise what housekeeping has not agreed.

## Build an answer sheet

The easiest improvement costs nothing. Write one page with the five answers above, plus anything specific to your property such as a steep drive, a one-way street or a gate code. Then:

1. Check every fact against your website and booking confirmation, so guests are not given two versions.
2. Mark which answers anyone may give and which need a manager.
3. Keep the page where front-of-house staff and anyone covering the phone can see it.
4. Update it when something changes, such as roadworks or a car-park closure.

If you use a phone tool, the same page is the source of truth you give it. Our [setup guide](https://gethanas.com/blog/how-to-set-up-an-ai-receptionist-at-a-small-hotel-in-one-week) walks through gathering those facts.

## Who covers the phone at check-in time

You have a few options, and they can be mixed:

- **Staff answer, and the desk waits.** Simple, but the guest in front of you waits while you speak to the guest on the phone.
- **A second person covers the phone** for a defined window. Effective, but it needs a second person.
- **Calls go to voicemail or a callback.** Cheapest, but guests on the way often hang up and book or look elsewhere.
- **A phone tool answers common questions** and passes the rest to staff. An AI receptionist is one version of this. If you try one, tell callers it is an AI, as in our [greeting guide](https://gethanas.com/blog/hotel-phone-greeting-what-to-say-and-why-an-ai-should-say-it-is-an-ai).

Some of this is in early access, so check what is live with any vendor before you plan around it. Whatever you choose, decide in advance which calls always go to a person, such as safety, access problems and complaints.

## A short arrival-day checklist

1. Answer sheet written and checked against the website.
2. Parking, early check-in, access and directions each have one approved answer.
3. A named person or fallback for locked-out and urgent calls.
4. Someone assigned to the phone during your busiest check-in hour.
5. Late arrivals and changes are logged where the next shift will see them.
6. Anything unusual goes to a manager.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most common question guests ask before arrival?

We have not measured this, and it will differ by property. Parking, early check-in and directions are common themes in general hotel practice, so start there and check your own call notes.

### Should I promise early check-in over the phone?

Only if the room is ready or you can say honestly when it will be. A firm "not before this time" is better than a hopeful maybe that leaves a guest waiting in the lobby.

### What if a guest is locked out?

Treat it as urgent and make sure it reaches a person. Give guests an emergency number in their confirmation message.

### Can an AI answer arrival-day calls?

It can answer factual questions you have given it, such as parking and directions, and pass the rest on. Test it with realistic calls first and keep a person available for anything unusual.

### How do I know if arrival-day calls are being missed?

Check your phone provider's call log for unanswered calls around check-in time, and compare them with your bookings for that day.
