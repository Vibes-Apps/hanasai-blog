## Why calls get missed at a B&B

In a hotel with a front desk, someone is paid to answer the phone. In a B&B or guest house, the person who would answer is usually also making breakfast, cleaning rooms, doing laundry, greeting arrivals or shopping for supplies. The phone rings at exactly the moments when your hands are full.

The common patterns are easy to recognise:

- **Breakfast service.** The kitchen and the dining room need you at the same time as the phone.
- **Cleaning and changeovers.** You are upstairs with a vacuum cleaner and a trolley.
- **A guest at the door.** Someone present always comes before someone on the phone, and rightly so.
- **You are out.** School run, shopping, a day off, a family event.
- **Evenings and early mornings.** People plan trips outside office hours, and many want to ask a quick question before they book.

We cannot give you a reliable figure for how many calls a B&B misses, and we have not seen a published one we would trust, so we do not quote one. Our article on [how many calls a small hotel misses](https://gethanas.com/blog/how-many-calls-does-a-small-hotel-miss-what-it-costs) explains how to measure your own: check your phone's missed-call log for a few weeks.

## What an AI receptionist can realistically do

An AI receptionist answers calls using a profile of your property. It does not know anything you have not told it. That is a strength and a limit.

**Answer routine questions from a profile.** Check-in and check-out times, breakfast hours, parking, dogs, directions, room types, whether rooms are en suite, and your cancellation policy. These are the questions that fill a B&B's phone, and the answers rarely change. With Hanas, the AI drafts a hotel profile from your website and your team reviews it. The site says missing details are flagged, not guessed.

**Take booking requests.** The AI collects dates, number of guests, room preference, a name and a contact number, then sends a request to you. On Hanas, requests arrive by email and in a dashboard, and staff approve or decline them. Its site says the AI does not confirm a booking and does not take payment, and that is how it works currently. For rates, you paste them in, and the receptionist quotes only those. If no rate applies, it takes details and asks you to call back.

**Answer in other languages.** Hanas lists demo calls in English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Russian and Arabic, and says it can switch to other languages when a caller changes language. It also says no language is guaranteed and that details are read back before a request is sent. Our article on [handling foreign-language callers](https://gethanas.com/blog/how-to-handle-foreign-language-callers-at-a-small-uk-hotel) goes into this in more detail.

**Answer at any hour.** Hanas says it answers 24/7. Whether it answers every call or only the ones you miss depends on how your phone number is set up, which the Hanas site does not spell out. Ask before you sign up.

## What it cannot do

This part matters more than the list above.

| Situation | What an AI receptionist can do | What it cannot do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guest standing at the door | Nothing | Be there, greet, carry bags, show the room |
| Emergency (fire, medical, flooding) | Nothing reliable | Act. Guests must call 999 or you directly |
| Complaint from a guest in the house | Take a message | Apologise in person, fix the problem, offer a refund |
| Unusual request ("can we bring a horse?") | Take details for you | Decide. Only you know what you will allow |
| Live availability | Take a request | See your calendar or booking sites (Hanas has no PMS or channel connection today) |
| Confirming and taking payment | Pass the request to you | Confirm or take a card, by default and currently on Hanas |

Three of these deserve a closer look.

**It is not in the house.** Guests staying with you will want a person. The AI answers the phone; it cannot knock on a door, hear a smoke alarm or notice that a guest looks unwell.

**Emergencies.** Guests should know to call 999, then you. Put that in the room information. Do not rely on an AI to handle one.

**Judgement.** A B&B runs on small decisions: a late arrival, an early breakfast for a walker. The AI can record the request. You decide the answer.

## A set-up checklist for a B&B

Most of the value comes from the setup rather than the technology. Work through these in order.

### 1. Write the profile

The AI is only as accurate as what you give it. Cover:

- Address, how to find you, parking and arrival instructions
- Check-in and check-out times, and your rule for late arrivals
- Room types, who each suits, and what is in them (en suite, bath, kettle, Wi-Fi)
- Breakfast times, what is included, and dietary options
- Pets, children, smoking and accessibility, stated plainly.

### 2. Decide when it answers

Decide whether you want the AI to answer all calls, or only when you are busy or closed. If you set it to cover only some hours, write those hours down: breakfast service, cleaning hours, evenings, days off. If the vendor cannot tell you how this works on your phone line, that is a reason to keep asking before you commit.

### 3. Decide who approves requests and how fast

Name one person for requests, and a backup. Set a response time you can keep: "within two hours during the day, by 9am for overnight requests" is realistic for many owners. Guests who are told staff will confirm should hear from you when they were told. In Hanas, nothing is confirmed to a guest until a person says yes. Our article on [why staff approval matters](https://gethanas.com/blog/should-an-ai-confirm-hotel-bookings-why-staff-approval-matters) explains the reasoning.

### 4. Tell your guests

Hanas says the AI discloses that it is an AI and that calls are recorded. Beyond that, say it on your website and booking confirmations so nothing comes as a surprise: "Our phone may be answered by an AI assistant, which takes messages and booking requests. We reply personally." Our article on [whether it is legal to use an AI receptionist at a UK hotel](https://gethanas.com/blog/is-it-legal-to-use-an-ai-receptionist-at-a-uk-hotel) covers the data protection side. It is not legal advice.

### 5. Test before you go live

Ring it yourself, from a phone that is not yours. Try a normal question, an awkward question, a booking request in a messy order, and a caller who changes their mind. Read the result that reaches you. Hanas offers a 14-day free trial of up to 60 minutes on your own phone number, whichever comes first, and dashboard test calls are free. A trial tells you more than any article, including this one.

### 6. Review regularly

Read a sample of calls weekly at first, and update rates and closures. Hanas deletes transcripts after 30 days by default, so review within that window.

## Who it fits, and who it does not

**It is more likely to suit you if:**

- you miss calls while you are working in the house, and you would rather return a call with the details already taken;
- you want to approve every request yourself;
- your guests include people who do not speak English as a first language;
- you take most bookings through booking sites and the phone is mainly for questions and the odd direct request.

**It is probably not for you if:**

- you want the phone to confirm bookings or take deposits without you;
- you rely on a booking system and want the AI to read availability or write bookings into it. Hanas does not connect to a booking system yet; Mews, Cloudbeds, Little Hotelier, Guestline and eviivo are planned, with no dates;
- you need someone to answer the phone and then actually do something, such as transfer to a mobile. Hanas does not state call transfer on its site, so check before you rely on it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will an AI receptionist replace me?

No. It covers calls you cannot take. You still approve requests, welcome guests and make every decision.

### Can it take a booking and confirm it?

On Hanas, no. It sends a request to you, and nothing is confirmed to the guest until you approve it. It does not take card details. Other vendors work differently, so check their pages.

### What if a guest calls with an emergency?

Do not rely on an AI. Guests should know to call 999 and then your direct number. Put that in the room information.

### Is it safe for guests' data?

Hanas says transcripts and requests are stored in Ireland, that transcripts are deleted after 30 days by default, and that its speech provider, ElevenLabs, processes calls in the US. Read the [privacy notice](https://gethanas.com/privacy) and decide for yourself. This is not legal advice.
